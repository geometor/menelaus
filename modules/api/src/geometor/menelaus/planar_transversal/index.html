src.geometor.menelaus.planar_transversal¶

Exact symbolic Planar Menelaus constructions, Converse theorems, and Rational Trigonometry.

Implements:

  • Classical Euclidean parallel-auxiliary demonstration of Planar Menelaus’s Theorem (Ptolemy Syntaxis Mathematica I.13, ed. Heiberg 1898 pp. 68–70; Halley 1758 Menelai Sphaericorum Libri III, Lemma I, p. 83), verifying both the directed ratio product \(\frac{\overline{AF}}{\overline{FB}} \cdot \frac{\overline{BD}}{\overline{DC}} \cdot \frac{\overline{CE}}{\overline{EA}} = -1\) and the unsigned magnitude product \(= +1\).

  • Universal symbolic polynomial proof over \(\mathbb{Q}(x_A, y_A, x_B, y_B, x_C, y_C, u, v, w)\) establishing both the forward theorem and the Converse of Menelaus.

  • Constructive applications of the Converse of Menelaus:

    1. External Angle-Bisector Axis (the three external angle bisectors of a scalene triangle meet the opposite sides in three collinear points; cf. Menelaus Sphaerica III.6).

    2. Desargues’s Two-Triangle Theorem (three Menelaus applications on \(\triangle OBC\), \(\triangle OCA\), \(\triangle OAB\)).

    3. Pascal’s Hexagrammum Mysticum (three Menelaus applications on the diagonal triangle combined with Euclid III.35/III.36 chord power products).

    4. Monge’s Three-Circle Similicenter Theorem (collinearity of the three exsimilicenters and three pairs of insimilicenters + one exsimilicenter).

  • Rational Trigonometry formulation (Wildberger Divine Proportions) expressing Planar Menelaus purely over \(\mathbb{Q}\) via quadrance, line_spread, vertex_spread, and cross_ratio without square roots or transcendental functions.

src.geometor.menelaus.planar_transversal.signed_segment_ratio(P: sympy.geometry.Point, Q: sympy.geometry.Point, R: sympy.geometry.Point) → sympy.Expr¶

Compute the exact directed ratio \(\overline{PR} / \overline{RQ}\) for collinear P, Q, R.

Under this classical tail-to-trace / trace-to-head convention:

  • signed_segment_ratio(P, Q, R) > 0 when R divides segment PQ internally (strictly between P and Q).

  • signed_segment_ratio(P, Q, R) < 0 when R divides line PQ externally (outside segment PQ).

Parameters:

  • P – Segment tail vertex.

  • Q – Segment head vertex.

  • R – Collinear division point (\(R \neq Q\)).

Returns:

Exact simplified sympy.Expr equal to \(\overline{PR} / \overline{RQ}\).

Raises:

ValueError – If P, Q, R are not collinear or R coincides with Q.

src.geometor.menelaus.planar_transversal.verify_planar_menelaus_universal() → dict[str, sympy.Expr]¶

Prove Planar Menelaus and its Converse universally over Q(xA, yA, xB, yB, xC, yC, u, v, w).

Parametrizes:

  • \(F = (1 - u)A + uB\) on \(AB\) (\(\overline{AF}/\overline{FB} = u/(1-u)\)),

  • \(D = (1 - w)B + wC\) on \(BC\) (\(\overline{BD}/\overline{DC} = w/(1-w)\)),

  • \(E = (1 - v)C + vA\) on \(CA\) (\(\overline{CE}/\overline{EA} = v/(1-v)\)).

Proves the exact polynomial determinant factorization:

\[\begin{split}\det\begin{pmatrix} x_D & y_D & 1 \\ x_E & y_E & 1 \\ x_F & y_F & 1 \end{pmatrix} = 2\,\operatorname{Area}(\triangle ABC)\,\bigl(u v w + (1 - u)(1 - v)(1 - w)\bigr),\end{split}\]

which vanishes for non-degenerate \(\triangle ABC\) if and only if \(\frac{u}{1-u}\frac{w}{1-w}\frac{v}{1-v} = -1\).

src.geometor.menelaus.planar_transversal.menelaus_rational_trig_invariants(model: geometor.model.Model, data: dict[str, Any]) → dict[str, Any]¶

Compute and verify Wildberger Rational Trigonometry invariants for Planar Menelaus.

Proves two purely rational (\(\mathbb{Q}\)-valued, square-root-free) formulations:

  1. Menelaus Quadrance Law: \(Q(A, F)\,Q(B, D)\,Q(C, E) = Q(F, B)\,Q(D, C)\,Q(E, A)\).

  2. Menelaus Spread Telescoping Law: Applying the Spread Law to sub-triangles \(\triangle AFE\), \(\triangle BDF\), and \(\triangle CDE\) cut by transversal \(t\) against side lines \(a, b, c\):

    \[\frac{Q(A, F)}{Q(E, A)} = \frac{s(t, b)}{s(t, c)},\quad \frac{Q(B, D)}{Q(F, B)} = \frac{s(t, c)}{s(t, a)},\quad \frac{Q(C, E)}{Q(D, C)} = \frac{s(t, a)}{s(t, b)},\]

    whose product telescopes identically to \(1\) over \(\mathbb{Q}\).

src.geometor.menelaus.planar_transversal.construct_desargues_axis(O_coords: tuple[Any, Any] = (0, 12), A_coords: tuple[Any, Any] = (-3, 7), B_coords: tuple[Any, Any] = (-1, 5), C_coords: tuple[Any, Any] = (4, 8), kA: sympy.Expr = sp.Rational(2, 1), kB: sympy.Expr = sp.Rational(12, 7), kC: sympy.Expr = sp.Rational(5, 2)) → tuple[geometor.model.Model, dict[str, Any]]¶

Prove Desargues’s Two-Triangle Theorem via three applications of Planar Menelaus.

Given center O and perspective triangles ABC and A1B1C1 along rays OA, OB, OC, applies Menelaus to:

  1. \(\triangle OBC\) with transversal \(B_1 C_1 D\),

  2. \(\triangle OCA\) with transversal \(C_1 A_1 E\),

  3. \(\triangle OAB\) with transversal \(A_1 B_1 F\),

multiplying all three identities to cancel the O-ray ratios and deduce \(\frac{\overline{AF}}{\overline{FB}} \cdot \frac{\overline{BD}}{\overline{DC}} \cdot \frac{\overline{CE}}{\overline{EA}} = -1\), establishing collinearity of D, E, F on Desargues’s axis by the Converse of Menelaus.

src.geometor.menelaus.planar_transversal.construct_pascal_hexagram() → tuple[geometor.model.Model, dict[str, Any]]¶

Prove Pascal’s Hexagrammum Mysticum on a circle via three Planar Menelaus applications.

Uses six exact integer points on the circle \(x^2 + y^2 = 100\): P1=(10,0), P2=(-6,8), P3=(0,-10), P4=(6,8), P5=(8,-6), P6=(-8,-6).

Forms diagonal triangle \(\triangle UVW\) from (P1P2, P3P4, P5P6):

  • U = P3P4 ∩ P5P6, V = P5P6 ∩ P1P2, W = P1P2 ∩ P3P4,

applies Planar Menelaus to \(\triangle UVW\) with transversals P2P3M, P4P5L, and P6P1N, cancels chord power products via Euclid III.35/III.36, and concludes by the Converse of Menelaus that opposite-side intersections L, M, N are collinear on Pascal’s line.

src.geometor.menelaus.planar_transversal.construct_monge_exsimilicenters(A_coords: tuple[Any, Any] = (0, 8), B_coords: tuple[Any, Any] = (-4, 0), C_coords: tuple[Any, Any] = (8, 0), rA: sympy.Expr = sp.Integer(1), rB: sympy.Expr = sp.Integer(2), rC: sympy.Expr = sp.Integer(4)) → tuple[geometor.model.Model, dict[str, Any]]¶

Prove Monge’s Three-Circle Theorem via the Converse of Menelaus.

Given circles centered at A, B, C with distinct exact positive radii rA, rB, rC, their external centers of similitude Sab, Sbc, Sca divide the center segments externally in ratios -rA/rB, -rB/rC, -rC/rA, whose product is identically -1, placing Sab, Sbc, Sca on Monge’s external axis of similitude! Also constructs the three internal similicenters Iab, Ibc, Ica to verify all four Monge axes of the complete quadrilateral.
