Proposition III.3 — Theorem: Rule of Four Quantities & Tangents¶

Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. III Prop. III. Theor. (Halley 1758, pp. 87–88)

Enunciation (Halley 1758, p. 87)

If two spherical triangles have two angles at either base right, and two other angles at the bases equal indeed yet not right: the sines of the two sides containing the right angle in one of the triangles shall be to one another in the ratio compounded of the ratio which the sines of the sides containing the right angle in the other triangle have to one another, taken in the same manner, and of the ratio which the sine of the arc intercepted between the vertex of the first triangle and the pole of its base has to the sine of the arc intercepted between the vertex of the other triangle and the pole of its base.

Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) and \(\triangle \mathrm{\Delta EZ}\) be two spherical triangles, of which the two angles

\(\angle \mathrm{A}, \angle \mathrm{\Delta}\) are right, while the two other angles at \(\mathrm{\Gamma}, \mathrm{Z}\) are equal yet not right, and let points \(\mathrm{H}, \mathrm{\Theta}\) be the poles of each base \(\mathrm{\Gamma A}, \mathrm{Z\Delta}\): I say that the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}\) is to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}\) in the ratio compounded of the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{E\Delta}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}\) and the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{HB}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{E\Theta}}\).

For let arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma K}}\) be made equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}\), and through \(\mathrm{H}\) the pole of arc \(\mathrm{A\Gamma}\) draw arc \(\mathrm{H\Lambda K}\) meeting arc \(\mathrm{\Gamma B}\) at \(\mathrm{\Lambda}\); and arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{K\Lambda}}\) will be equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}\), as \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda H}}\) is to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{E\Theta}}\). Now because figure \(\mathrm{AH\Lambda\Gamma}\) is after the manner of the figure of Proposition I of this book, the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}\) will be to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{BH}}\) in the ratio compounded of the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma K}}\), and the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{K\Lambda}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda H}}\): whence by transposing the terms, the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}\) will be compounded of the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{BH}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda H}}\), and the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{K\Lambda}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma K}}\). But arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma K}}\) is equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Delta}}\), and arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{K\Lambda}}\) to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}\), just as arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda H}}\) is to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta E}}\): wherefore the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}\) is compounded of the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Delta}}\) and the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{BH}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta E}}\). Quod erat demonstrandum.

Corollary. Hence it is established that the tangents of the perpendiculars \(\mathrm{AB}, \mathrm{\Delta E}\) are proportional to the sines of the bases \(\mathrm{A\Gamma}, \mathrm{\Delta Z}\):

\[\frac{\tan \widehat{\mathrm{AB}}}{\tan \widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}} \;=\; \frac{\sin \widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}}{\sin \widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}}.\]
