Analytical Summary: The Orthic Axis & Triple Menelaus Proof¶

Modern Elements-2.0 Decomposition of Proposition III.10

1. Deductive Architecture of Menelaus’s Masterpiece¶

Proposition III.10 is widely regarded as one of the supreme technical summits of Hellenistic geometry:

  1. The Orthic Axis / Trilinear Polar: Menelaus constructs the great-circle arc \(E\Delta\Theta\) through the altitude feet \(\Delta\) and \(E\), meeting base \(A\Gamma\) produced at \(\Theta\). In projective geometry, \(E\Delta\Theta\) is the trilinear polar of the altitude concurrence \(Z\).

  2. Triple Application of Proposition III.1: Applying the spherical Menelaus theorem to three distinct configurations (\(A\Theta EZ\), \(A\Gamma BZ\), and \(AB\Gamma Z\)) cancels intermediate terms, proving that \(\Theta\) and \(H\) divide \(A\Gamma\) harmonically in sines:

    \[\frac{\sin\widehat{A\Theta}}{\sin\widehat{\Theta\Gamma}} \;=\; \frac{\sin\widehat{AH}}{\sin\widehat{H\Gamma}}.\]

  3. Incenter of the Orthic Triangle: Using the second converse of Prop. III.8, Menelaus proves that the altitudes \(\Delta Z\) and \(EZ\) bisect the internal angles of the orthic triangle \(\triangle \Delta EH\). By Prop. III.9, the third ray \(HZ\) must bisect \(\angle EH\Delta\).

  4. Deduction of the Third Altitude: Applying Prop. III.6 and the first converse of Prop. III.8, the harmonic division forces the third angle \(\angle AHB\) to be a right angle, proving \(BH \perp A\Gamma\)!
