Proposition III.10 — Concurrency of Spherical Altitudes¶

Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. III Prop. X. Theor. (Halley 1758, pp. 95–97)

Proposition III.10 — Theorem: Spherical Orthocenter Concurrency (pp. 95–97)¶

Enunciation (Halley 1758, p. 95)

If from any two angles of a spherical triangle two perpendicular arcs be dropped onto the sides opposite to them: the arc drawn from the remaining angle through the point where those first two arcs meet, when produced, shall also be perpendicular to the remaining side.

[§4.4 · Menelaus’s Masterpiece Proof via Triple Menelaus & Orthic Harmonic Polarity, pp. 95–97] Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) be a spherical triangle, and from vertices \(\mathrm{A}\) and \(\mathrm{\Gamma}\) drop perpendicular arcs \(\mathrm{A\Delta} \perp \mathrm{B\Gamma}\) and \(\mathrm{\Gamma E} \perp \mathrm{AB}\) meeting at \(\mathrm{Z}\); join \(\mathrm{BZ}\) and produce it to meet \(\mathrm{A\Gamma}\) at \(\mathrm{H}\) (crossing \(\mathrm{E\Delta}\) at \(\mathrm{K}\)). Menelaus claims \(\mathrm{BH} \perp \mathrm{A\Gamma}\):

  1. Constructing the Orthic Axis (Trilinear Polar): Draw the great-circle arc \(\mathrm{E\Delta}\) through the feet \(\mathrm{\Delta}, \mathrm{E}\) of the two altitudes, produce it to meet \(\mathrm{A\Gamma}\) produced at \(\mathrm{\Theta}\), and join \(\mathrm{H\Delta}\) and \(\mathrm{HE}\).

  2. Triple Application of Proposition III.1 (*Theorema Menelai*): - In Menelaus figure \(\mathrm{A\Theta EZ}\), \(\sin \widehat{\mathrm{A\Theta}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{\Theta\Gamma}} = (\sin \widehat{\mathrm{A\Delta}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}) \cdot (\sin \widehat{\mathrm{ZE}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{E\Gamma}})\). - In Menelaus figure \(\mathrm{A\Gamma BZ}\), \(\sin \widehat{\mathrm{AH}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{H\Gamma}} = (\sin \widehat{\mathrm{AZ}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{Z\Delta}}) \cdot (\sin \widehat{\mathrm{\Delta B}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}})\). - In Menelaus figure \(\mathrm{AB\Gamma Z}\), \(\sin \widehat{\mathrm{\Delta B}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}} = (\sin \widehat{\mathrm{\Delta A}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{AZ}}) \cdot (\sin \widehat{\mathrm{ZE}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{E\Gamma}})\).

    Substituting the third ratio into the second cancels \(\sin \widehat{\mathrm{AZ}}\), leaving the exact same compounded ratio as the first:

    \[\frac{\sin \widehat{\mathrm{A\Theta}}}{\sin \widehat{\mathrm{\Theta\Gamma}}} \;=\; \frac{\sin \widehat{\mathrm{A\Delta}}}{\sin \widehat{\mathrm{Z\Delta}}} \cdot \frac{\sin \widehat{\mathrm{ZE}}}{\sin \widehat{\mathrm{E\Gamma}}} \;=\; \frac{\sin \widehat{\mathrm{AH}}}{\sin \widehat{\mathrm{H\Gamma}}}.\]

    Thus \(\mathrm{\Theta}\) and \(\mathrm{H}\) divide base \(\mathrm{A\Gamma}\) harmonically in sines!

  3. Orthic Triangle Bisectors via Prop. III.8 & III.9: Because \(\angle \mathrm{A\Delta\Gamma}\) is right, the second converse of Proposition III.8 gives \(\angle \mathrm{\Theta\Delta\Gamma} = \angle \mathrm{\Gamma\Delta H}\) (hence \(\angle \mathrm{A\Delta H} = \angle \mathrm{A\Delta E}\)); likewise at right angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma EA}\), \(\angle \mathrm{\Delta E\Gamma} = \angle \mathrm{\Gamma EH}\). Therefore, in the orthic spherical triangle \(\triangle \mathrm{\Delta EH}\), the altitudes \(\mathrm{\Delta Z}\) and \(\mathrm{EZ}\) bisect angles \(\angle \mathrm{\Delta}\) and \(\angle \mathrm{E}\)! By Proposition III.9, \(\mathrm{HZ}\) also bisects \(\angle \mathrm{EH\Delta}\)!

  4. Right Angle at H via Prop. III.6 & Converse I of Prop. III.8: Applying Proposition III.6 to \(\triangle \mathrm{\Theta\Delta H}\) and \(\triangle \mathrm{\Theta EH}\) (whose angles at \(\mathrm{\Delta}\) and \(\mathrm{E}\) are bisected by \(\mathrm{\Delta\Gamma}\) and \(\mathrm{E\Gamma}\)) yields \(\sin \widehat{\mathrm{\Theta E}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{\Theta\Delta}} = \sin \widehat{\mathrm{EH}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{H\Delta}} = \sin \widehat{\mathrm{EK}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{K\Delta}}\) (since \(\mathrm{HZK}\) bisects \(\angle \mathrm{EH\Delta}\)). Since \(\angle \mathrm{EHK} = \angle \mathrm{\Delta HK}\) AND \((\mathrm{\Theta}, \mathrm{K}; \mathrm{E}, \mathrm{\Delta})\) is harmonic in sines, the first converse of Proposition III.8 forces \(\angle \mathrm{\Theta HK}\) (i.e., \(\angle \mathrm{AHB}\)) to be a right angle! Q.E.D.
