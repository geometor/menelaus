Scholarly & GEOMETOR Notes: External Bisector Axis & Trilinear Polarity¶

Planar and Spherical Connections in geometor.menelaus

1. Planar Counterpart: External Angle-Bisector Axis¶

The planar version of Proposition III.6 states that the internal and external bisectors of a triangle divide the sides in the ratio of the adjacent sides (Euclid Elements VI.3).

In construct_external_bisector_axis(), on Heronian △ABC (\(a=7, b=20, c=15\), incenter I), the external bisector feet De, Ee, Fe satisfy:

\[\left(-\frac{c}{a}\right) \left(-\frac{a}{b}\right) \left(-\frac{b}{c}\right) \;=\; -1.\]

By the Converse of the Menelaus Theorem, the three external bisector feet are collinear, forming the anti-orthic axis—the trilinear polar of the incenter \(I = X(1)\).

2. Role in Menelaus’s Deductive Progression¶

Proposition III.6 is the essential engine that drives the second half of Book III: - Prop. III.8: Combines internal and external bisectors at a right angle to establish harmonic division. - Prop. III.9: Applies internal bisectors to prove the concurrence of angle bisectors at the incenter. - Prop. III.10: Uses Prop. III.6 and its converse to prove that the three altitudes concur at the orthocenter.
