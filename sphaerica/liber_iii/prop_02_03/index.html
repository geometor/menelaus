Propositions III.2 & III.3 — Spherical Law of Sines & Rule of Tangents¶

Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. III Props. II & III (Halley 1758, pp. 86–88)

Proposition III.1 Coroll. 2 — Right-Angled Spherical Triangles (p. 86)¶

When \(\mathrm{\Gamma}\) is the pole of \(\mathrm{AB}\) and \(\mathrm{B}\) is the pole of \(\mathrm{A\Gamma}\), angles \(\mathrm{A}, \mathrm{E}, \mathrm{\Delta}\) are right angles and all four arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}, \widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}, \widehat{\mathrm{BE}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}\) are quadrants. Triangles \(\triangle \mathrm{\Gamma EZ}\) and \(\triangle \mathrm{B\Delta Z}\) are right-angled spherical triangles whose angles at \(\mathrm{\Gamma}\) and \(\mathrm{B}\) are measured by arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{AE}}\) and \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Delta}}\) respectively. From this corollary, Halley notes, the canons for resolving almost all cases of spherical trigonometry may be deduced without difficulty by dropping perpendiculars to resolve oblique triangles into right-angled ones.

Proposition III.2 — Theorem: The Spherical Law of Sines (pp. 86–87)¶

Enunciation (Halley 1758, p. 86)

If of two spherical triangles two angles be equal, and two other angles be either equal to one another or taken together equal to two right angles: I say that the sines of the sides subtending the two equal angles are to the sines of the sides subtending the two other angles (whether equal or supplementary) respectively in the same ratio; and conversely.

[§3.6 · Demonstration & Consequence, Halley pp. 86–87] Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) and \(\triangle \mathrm{\Delta EZ}\) be two spherical triangles such that angle \(\angle \mathrm{A}\) of the one equals angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Delta}\) of the other, and angles \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma}\) and \(\angle \mathrm{Z}\) are either equal or supplementary: I say that \(\sin \widehat{\mathrm{AB}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}} = \sin \widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{EZ}}\).

By completing the auxiliary triangle configuration and producing the sides to meet at \(\mathrm{K}\), Menelaus applies Proposition III.1 to establish the proportion. Setting \(\triangle \mathrm{\Delta EZ}\) to be the mirror or perpendicular sub-triangle of \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) itself immediately yields the universal Spherical Law of Sines:

\[\frac{\sin a}{\sin A} \;=\; \frac{\sin b}{\sin B} \;=\; \frac{\sin c}{\sin C}.\]

Proposition III.3 & Corollary — Rule of Four Quantities & Tangents (p. 88)¶

f. halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0104 · p. 88

Enunciation & Corollary (Halley 1758, p. 88)

In two right-angled spherical triangles \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) (right at \(\mathrm{A}\)) and \(\triangle \mathrm{\Delta EZ}\) (right at \(\mathrm{\Delta}\)) sharing an equal acute angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma} = \angle \mathrm{Z}\): I say that \(\sin \widehat{\mathrm{AB}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}\) is compounded of \(\sin \widehat{\mathrm{E\Delta}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}\) and \(\sin \widehat{\mathrm{HB}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{E\Theta}}\). Corollary: Hence it is manifest that the tangents of the perpendicular legs \(\mathrm{AB}, \mathrm{\Delta E}\) are proportional to the sines of the bases \(\mathrm{A\Gamma}, \mathrm{\Delta Z}\) (\(\tan \widehat{\mathrm{AB}} : \tan \widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}} = \sin \widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}\)).
