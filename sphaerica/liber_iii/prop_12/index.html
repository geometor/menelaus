Proposition III.12 — Theorem: Right-Angled Triangle Side & Base Ratios¶

Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. III Prop. XII (Halley 1758, pp. 99–101)

Enunciation (Halley 1758, p. 99)

If one of the angles at the base of a spherical triangle be acute, and the other right; and the side subtending the right angle be not greater than a quadrant of a circle; and on this side two arcs be taken, from whose endpoints arcs be drawn perpendicular to the base, and these arcs taken on the side be equal: the arcs which are intercepted between those perpendiculars shall be unequal; and the greater of them shall lie adjacent to the right angle. And in this case all the other relations which we have described in the preceding propositions shall also come to pass.

Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) be a spherical triangle, whose angle \(\angle \mathrm{B}\) is acute, and angle \(\angle \mathrm{A}\) right; and let side \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) not be greater than a quadrant of a circle;

and on side \(\mathrm{B\Gamma}\) let two arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}, \widehat{\mathrm{Z\Theta}}\) be taken, through whose endpoints arcs \(\mathrm{\Delta E}, \mathrm{ZH}, \mathrm{\Theta K}\) are drawn at right angles upon base \(\mathrm{AB}\): I say that if arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}, \widehat{\mathrm{Z\Theta}}\) be equal, arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AE}}\) will be greater than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{HK}}\); and if the two arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{AE}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}\) taken together be equal to the two \(\widehat{\mathrm{HK}}, \widehat{\mathrm{Z\Theta}}\) taken together, arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}\) will be less than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Theta}}\): and if the difference of arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{AE}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}\) be equal to the difference between arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta Z}}, \widehat{\mathrm{KH}}\) (taking away the less from the greater), arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}\) will be greater than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Theta}}\). And universally the ratio which arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AE}}\) has to \(\widehat{\mathrm{HK}}\) will be greater than the ratio of \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}\) to \(\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Theta}}\).

For since arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) is less than a quadrant of a circle, and the angle at \(\mathrm{B}\) is acute, while the angles at \(\mathrm{A}, \mathrm{E}, \mathrm{H}, \mathrm{K}\) are right; (by Book III Prop. 5) as the sine of the sum of arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}, \widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) is to the sine of their difference, so is the sine of the sum of arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta B}}, \widehat{\mathrm{BE}}\) to the sine of the difference of arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta B}}, \widehat{\mathrm{BE}}\); and so the sine of the sum of arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{ZB}}, \widehat{\mathrm{BH}}\) to the sine of their difference, and finally so the sine of the sum of arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta B}}, \widehat{\mathrm{BK}}\) to the sine of their difference; and since these things are so, all those relations stated in the premises will come to pass.

Again, if arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) be a quadrant of a circle, so that \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}\) is equal to \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\), the very same things will follow in this case also, according to what we demonstrated in the first part of the Book of Cyclical Lemmas.

But the same things will also be proved in another way out of this third Book. For let arcs \(\mathrm{A\Gamma}, \mathrm{E\Delta}, \mathrm{HZ}, \mathrm{K\Theta}\) be produced to the pole of arc \(\mathrm{AB}\), which let be point \(\mathrm{\Lambda}\); and (by Book III Prop. 3) the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{BE}}\) will be compounded of the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{E\Delta}}\)—that is, of the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Delta}}\)—and of the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda\Delta}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda\Gamma}}\). But \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda\Delta}}\) is greater than \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda\Gamma}}\), and each is less than a quadrant of a circle; wherefore the ratio of the sine of \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{BE}}\) is greater

than the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Delta}}\). In like manner it will be evident that the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{EB}}\) to the sine of \(\widehat{\mathrm{BK}}\) is greater than the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta B}}\) to the sine of \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Theta}}\). Hence it can be established that the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{EK}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{KA}}\) is less than the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta\Theta}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta\Gamma}}\): therefore invertendo the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{KA}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{KE}}\) is greater than the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta\Gamma}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta\Delta}}\). And in the same way it will be proved that the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{EK}}\) has a greater ratio to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{KH}}\) than the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta\Theta}}\) has to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta Z}}\). Likewise, since the ratio of the sine of \(\widehat{\mathrm{HB}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{BK}}\) is greater than the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{ZB}}\) to the sine of \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Theta}}\), the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{KA}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AH}}\) will be less than the ratio of the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta\Gamma}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma Z}}\); and therefore the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AH}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AE}}\) will be in a less ratio than the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma Z}}\) to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}\). Now since these things are so, all those relations stated in this proposition will come to pass, and arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AE}}\) will be to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{HK}}\) in a greater ratio than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}\) is to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Theta}}\). Quod erat demonstrandum.

Scholion (Halley 1758, p. 101). “In this, as in the preceding proposition, there is cited a book whose title is ספר התמונות ההקשיות, that is, the Book of Cyclical Propositions or rather Lemmata, as the words seem rightly rendered. Brief indeed and imperfect are the arguments adduced for demonstrating these matters and drawn from the said book, so that of what character it was one cannot even guess by conjecture. Yet from the agreement of both manuscript codices I should believe the same things were formerly found in the lost Greek text of the Author himself: though whether that text reached the translators whole, or rather mutilated in this part, I would scarce dare decide. Nor let it trouble you if some things be here found wanting; for in the Corollaries to the fifteenth proposition of this third Book you will find the very same things demonstrated somewhat more clearly.”
