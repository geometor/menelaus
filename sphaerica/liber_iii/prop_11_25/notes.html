Scholarly & GEOMETOR Notes: The Mathematical Legacy of Book III¶

Reception History from Alexandria to the Scientific Revolution

1. The Islamic Astronomical Tradition¶

In the medieval Islamic world, Book III of Menelaus’s Sphaerica was translated, studied, and commented upon by the leading mathematicians of the 9th through 13th centuries:

  • Ishāq ibn Hunayn & Thābit ibn Qurra (9th c.): Produced the foundational Arabic edition (Kitāb al-Ukar).

  • Al-Battānī (Opus Astronomicum, c. 900): Applied Menelaus’s theorems to compute precise astronomical tables.

  • Abū Naṣr Manṣūr (c. 1007): Dedicated an entire treatise (Kitāb fī Maʿrifat Qisiyy al-Kura) to the Sphaerica, proving the Spherical Law of Sines autonomously.

  • Naṣīr al-Dīn al-Ṭūsī (1201–1274): Author of the Kitāb al-Shakl al-Qattāʿ (Treatise on the Transversal Figure), the first standalone textbook on spherical trigonometry.

2. European Renaissance & Oxford Publication¶

  • Regiomontanus (De Triangulis Omnimodis, printed Nuremberg 1533): Relied heavily on the medieval Latin translations of Menelaus by Gerard of Cremona.

  • Edmond Halley (Oxford 1758): Restored the text from Bodleian Arabic and Hebrew manuscripts, providing the definitive scholarly edition of the 18th century.

  • Axel Anthon Björnbo (Leipzig 1902): Completed the definitive modern stemmatic study (Studien über Menelaos’ Sphärik), reconstructing the transmission history across Greek, Arabic, Hebrew, and Latin witnesses.
