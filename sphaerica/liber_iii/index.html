Book III: The Transversal Theorem, Sines & Harmonic Pencils¶

Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Liber Tertius — Edmond Halley (1758, pp. 80–112) & Claudius Ptolemy (1898, pp. 68–75)

Book III is the mathematical pinnacle of the Sphaerica and the foundational cornerstone of spherical trigonometry in world mathematics. Where Books I and II establish the synthetic geometry of spherical triangles and rising-time inequalities, Book III develops the exact quantitative chord/sine metric:

  1. The Menelaus Transversal Theorem (*Theorema Menelai* / *Figura Catha*, Prop. III.1, Halley pp. 80–82): Transfers the six-segment complete quadrilateral ratio across great circles via central gnomonic projection from the sphere center to planar chord triangles.

  2. Foundations of Trigonometry (Lemmata I–II & Props. III.2–III.5): Establishes the Planar Menelaus Theorem, the Chord-Sine projection lemma, the right-spherical-triangle canon, the Spherical Law of Sines, and the Rule of Four Quantities and Tangents.

  3. Harmonic Pencils & Concurrencies (Props. III.6–III.10): Proves the internal and external angle-bisector theorem in sines, spherical harmonic division and 4-ray cross-ratio pencil invariance, the concurrence of angle bisectors (incenter), and the crowning three-transversal proof of the concurrency of spherical altitudes (orthocenter) fifteen centuries before Ceva and Poncelet.

  4. Astronomical Rising Times (Props. III.11–III.15): Concludes with exact arc-difference ratio bounds and small-circle extremum inequalities (numbered III.11–III.25 in Abū Naṣr Manṣūr’s Arabic redaction) governing ancient and medieval spherical astronomy.

Proposition III.2 — Theorem: The Spherical Law of Sines

Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) and \(\triangle \mathrm{\Delta EZ}\) be two spherical triangles, and let angle \(\angle \mathrm{A}\) of the one be equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Delta}\) of the other; and let the two angles \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma}\) and \(\angle \mathrm{Z}\) either be equal to one another or together equal to two right angles: I say that the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}\) is to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) as the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}\) is to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{EZ}}\).

Proposition III.11 — Theorem: Base Arc Differences & Ratio Bounds

Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) be a spherical triangle, whose leg \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) is greater than the remaining leg \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma A}}\), yet not greater than a quadrant of a circle; and on \(\mathrm{B\Gamma}\) let arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}, \widehat{\mathrm{Z\Theta}}\) be taken, and through their endpoints let arcs \(\mathrm{\Delta E}, \mathrm{ZH}, \mathrm{\Theta K}\) be drawn containing with the base angles equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{A}\): I say that if arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}\) be equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Theta}}\), then \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Lambda}}\)—the difference between arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma A}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}\)—is less than \(\widehat{\mathrm{ZM}}\), the difference of \(\widehat{\mathrm{ZH}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Theta K}}\): if the differences between the said arcs be equal, arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}\) is greater than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Theta}}\). And if arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}\) together with the excess by which \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma A}}\) exceeds \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}\) be equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Theta}}\) together with the difference of \(\widehat{\mathrm{ZH}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Theta K}}\), then arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}\) is also greater than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Theta}}\). But if the difference between arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}\) and \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Lambda}}\) (by which namely \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma A}}\) exceeds \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}\)) be equal to the difference between \(\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Theta}}\) and \(\widehat{\mathrm{ZM}}\) (by which \(\widehat{\mathrm{ZH}}\) exceeds \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta K}}\)), then arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}\) will be less than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Theta}}\). Finally, I say that the ratio of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}\) to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Theta}}\) is greater than the ratio of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Lambda}}\) to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{ZM}}\).

Proposition III.13 — Theorem: Oblique-Ascension Differences

Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) be a spherical triangle, and let side \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}\) be greater than \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma B}}\), yet not greater than a quadrant of a circle; and on \(\mathrm{B\Gamma}\) let arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}\) be taken, from whose endpoints let arcs be drawn to base \(\mathrm{AB}\) containing with it angles equal to the angle at \(\mathrm{A}\) on the same side, such as arcs \(\mathrm{\Delta E}, \mathrm{ZH}\); as also arcs \(\mathrm{\Gamma\Theta}, \mathrm{\Delta K}, \mathrm{Z\Lambda}\) perpendicular to base \(\mathrm{AB}\): I say that if arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AE}}\) be equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{EH}}\), arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta K}}\) will be less than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{K\Lambda}}\); and if arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta K}}\) be equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{K\Lambda}}\), arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AE}}\) will be greater than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{EH}}\); and the other things just stated will come to pass. And universally the ratio which arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AE}}\) has to \(\widehat{\mathrm{EH}}\) will be greater than the ratio of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta K}}\) to \(\widehat{\mathrm{K\Lambda}}\).

Proposition III.15 — Theorem: Final Arc-Sine & Small-Circle Bounds (Finis)

Part I — Fundamental Rectangle of Semidiameters (p. 108). Let \(\mathrm{AB}, \mathrm{B\Gamma}\) be two great circles inclined to one another, and on \(\mathrm{AB}\) let two points \(\mathrm{\Delta}, \mathrm{E}\) be taken, through which let perpendiculars \(\mathrm{\Delta\Gamma}, \mathrm{EH}\) be drawn to arc \(\mathrm{B\Gamma}\): I say that the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma H}}\) is to the sine of arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{E\Delta}}\), as the rectangle under the semidiameter of the sphere and the semidiameter of the circle parallel to \(\mathrm{B\Gamma}\) and touching circle \(\mathrm{BA}\) is to the rectangle under the semidiameters of the circles passing through points \(\mathrm{\Delta}, \mathrm{E}\) and parallel to circle \(\mathrm{B\Gamma}\).
