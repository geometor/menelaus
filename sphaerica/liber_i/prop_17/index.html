Proposition I.17 — Theorem: AAA Spherical Congruence¶

Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. I Prop. XVII (Halley 1758, pp. 18–19)

Enunciation (Halley 1758, p. 18)

If two spherical triangles have the three angles of the one equal to the three angles of the other, each to its corresponding angle: then the arcs containing those triangles are also equal to one another, each to its corresponding side.

[§1.5 · Menelaus’s Direct Intrinsic Demonstration] Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) and \(\triangle \mathrm{\Delta EZ}\) be two spherical triangles in which angle \(\angle \mathrm{A}\) is equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Delta}\), angle \(\angle \mathrm{B}\) to angle \(\angle \mathrm{E}\), and angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma}\) to angle \(\angle \mathrm{Z}\): I say that arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}\) is equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}\), arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{EZ}}\), and arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}\) to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}\).

Produce arc \(\mathrm{AB}\) past \(\mathrm{B}\) to \(\mathrm{I}\), and make \(\widehat{\mathrm{BI}}\) equal to \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}\); and produce \(\mathrm{\Gamma B}\) past \(\mathrm{B}\) to \(\mathrm{\Theta}\), and make \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Theta}}\) equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{EZ}}\); and draw the great-circle arc \(\mathrm{I\Theta}\), which produce to meet arc \(\mathrm{A\Gamma}\) also produced at point \(\mathrm{K}\).

  1. Now the two arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta B}}, \widehat{\mathrm{BI}}\) are equal to the two arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}, \widehat{\mathrm{EZ}}\), and angle \(\angle \mathrm{IB\Theta}\) equals the vertical angle \(\angle \mathrm{AB\Gamma} = \angle \mathrm{E}\): wherefore arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{I\Theta}}\) is equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}\), and angle \(\angle \mathrm{I}\) to angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Delta}\) (which is equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{A}\)); wherefore angle \(\angle \mathrm{I}\) is equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{A}\). And angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Theta}\) is equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{Z}\) (which is equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma}\)); so that angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Theta}\) is equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma}\).

  2. And since the exterior angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma}\) of triangle \(\triangle \mathrm{\Theta K\Gamma}\) is equal to the interior and remote angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Theta}\), the two arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta K}}\) and \(\widehat{\mathrm{K\Gamma}}\) taken together will be equal to a semicircle (by Proposition I.10).

  3. In like manner, since the exterior angle \(\angle \mathrm{I}\) of triangle \(\triangle \mathrm{AIK}\) is equal to the remote and interior angle \(\angle \mathrm{A}\), the two arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{AK}}\) and \(\widehat{\mathrm{KI}}\) will also be equal to a semicircle (by the same Proposition I.10).

  4. And accordingly the two arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{K\Theta}} + \widehat{\mathrm{K\Gamma}}\) are equal to the two arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{IK}} + \widehat{\mathrm{KA}}\). Let the common arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{IK}}\) and \(\widehat{\mathrm{K\Gamma}}\) be subtracted from both sides, and the remaining arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta I}}\) will be equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}\)!

  5. But arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta I}}\) is equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Delta}}\); wherefore arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Delta}}\) is equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}\). And angle \(\angle \mathrm{A}\) is equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Delta}\), as angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma}\) is to angle \(\angle \mathrm{Z}\): wherefore (by Proposition I.14, ASA) arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}\) is equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}\), and arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{EZ}}\).

Quod erat demonstrandum.
