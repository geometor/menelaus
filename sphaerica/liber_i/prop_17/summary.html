Analytical Summary: AAA Congruence & Scale Invariance Failure¶

Deductive Architecture of Proposition I.17

  1. Failure of Planar Similarity (Contrast Euclid Elements VI.4): In Euclidean planar geometry, two equiangular triangles are similar (\(\triangle ABC \sim \triangle DEF\)), but not necessarily congruent: their sides are merely proportional, allowing arbitrary scale magnification or dilation by a continuous parameter \(k \in \mathbb{R}_{> 0}\). On the sphere \(S^2\), scale invariance fails completely: two equiangular triangles must be strictly congruent!

  2. Menelaus’s Primal Sphere Construction: Rather than appealing to polar triangle duality, Menelaus executes a direct intrinsic proof: - Reflects \(\triangle \Delta EZ\) vertically across vertex \(B\) by setting \(BI = \Delta E\) and \(B\Theta = EZ\). - Produces \(I\Theta\) to meet side \(A\Gamma\) produced at \(K\). - By SAS congruence (Proposition I.4), \(\triangle BI\Theta \cong \triangle E\Delta Z\), giving \(I\Theta = \Delta Z\), \(\angle I = \angle A\), and \(\angle \Theta = \angle \Gamma\). - Applying Proposition I.10 to \(\triangle \Theta K\Gamma\) forces \(K\Theta + K\Gamma = \pi\). - Applying Proposition I.10 to \(\triangle AIK\) forces \(AK + KI = \pi\). - Equating the two semicircle sums and subtracting common arcs leaves \(\Theta I = A\Gamma\) identically, forcing \(A\Gamma = \Delta Z\) and yielding full congruence by ASA (Proposition I.14)!
