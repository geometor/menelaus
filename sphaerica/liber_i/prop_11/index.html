Proposition I.11 — Theorem: Spherical Angle-Sum Excess¶

Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. I Prop. XI (Halley 1758, p. 11)

Enunciation (Halley 1758, p. 11)

In every spherical triangle, when one side is produced, the exterior angle is less than both remote interior angles taken together; and the three interior angles of the triangle taken together are greater than two right angles.

[§1.4 · Demonstration] Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) be a spherical triangle: I say that the exterior angle contained by arcs \(\mathrm{B\Gamma}\) and \(\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}\) is less than angles \(\mathrm{A}\) and \(\mathrm{B}\) opposite to it; and that the three angles of the triangle \(\mathrm{A}, \mathrm{B}, \mathrm{\Gamma}\) taken together exceed two right angles.

At point \(\mathrm{\Gamma}\) on arc \(\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}\) let angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Delta\Gamma E}\) be made equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{A}\), and produce \(\mathrm{AB}\) to meet \(\mathrm{A\Gamma}\) produced at point \(\mathrm{\Delta}\).

Now since angles \(\angle \mathrm{\Delta}\) and \(\angle \mathrm{\Delta\Gamma E}\) are equal, arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}\) and \(\widehat{\mathrm{E\Gamma}}\) will also be equal; and \(\widehat{\mathrm{BE}}\) and \(\widehat{\mathrm{E\Gamma}}\) together are equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Delta}}\), and therefore are strictly less than a semicircle (since \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}} + \widehat{\mathrm{B\Delta}}\) is a semicircle, and \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}} > 0\)).

Wherefore (by the preceding Proposition I.10) the exterior angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma BA}\) is greater than the interior angle \(\angle \mathrm{B\Gamma E}\). And consequently angle \(\angle \mathrm{B\Gamma\Delta}\) exterior to triangle \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) is less than angles \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma BA}\) and \(\angle \mathrm{BA\Gamma}\) taken together.

Let the common interior angle \(\angle \mathrm{B\Gamma A}\) be added to both sides: then the two angles \(\angle \mathrm{B\Gamma\Delta}\) and \(\angle \mathrm{B\Gamma A}\) are less than the three angles \(\angle \mathrm{A}, \angle \mathrm{B}, \angle \mathrm{\Gamma}\). But the two angles \(\angle \mathrm{B\Gamma\Delta}\) and \(\angle \mathrm{B\Gamma A}\) are together equal to two right angles (being adjacent on a semicircle):

Wherefore the three angles \(\angle \mathrm{A}, \angle \mathrm{B}, \angle \mathrm{\Gamma}\) taken together strictly exceed two right angles:

\[A + B + C \;>\; \pi.\]

Quod erat demonstrandum.
