Book I: Intrinsic Spherical Triangles, Congruence & Excess¶

Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Liber Primus — Edmond Halley (1758, pp. [i]–[viii], 1–47)

In Book I, Menelaus of Alexandria creates the intrinsic axiomatic science of spherical trigonometry on \(S^2\). Moving decisively beyond the three-dimensional solid-geometry and plane-slicing methods of Theodosius of Bithynia, Menelaus defines a spherical triangle (triangulum sphaericum) directly upon the curved surface of the sphere as a space enclosed by three arcs of great circles, each strictly less than a semicircle (circulus magnus).

Through an elegant sequence of direct constructions and demonstrations—avoiding reductio ad absurdum and planar superposition fallacies—Menelaus proves:

  • Copying Spherical Angles (Proposition I.1): polar circle construction without straightedge superposition.

  • Congruence Theorems (Propositions I.4, I.8, I.9, I.10, and I.17): SAS, SSS, ASA, and the celebrated AAA Spherical Congruence Theorem, establishing that on a positively curved surface, equiangular triangles are strictly congruent with zero non-congruent scale dilation.

  • Spherical Triangle Inequality (Proposition I.5): any two great-circle arcs together exceed the third.

  • Spherical Angle-Sum Excess (Proposition I.11): the historic first rigorous demonstration that the angle sum of an intrinsic spherical triangle strictly exceeds two right angles (\(A + B + C > \pi\)).

Preface to the Kind Reader (Praefatio Costardi)

[§1.1 · Halley 1758, pp. i–ii] Euclid, who leads the column of mathematicians—edited in 1703 by the most learned David Gregory, Savilian Professor of Astronomy—was followed in 1706 by the books of Apollonius of Perga On Cutting Off a Ratio and a Space, translated into Latin and restored from an Arabic manuscript by a man beyond all praise, Edmond Halley, Gregory’s closest colleague; and in the very next year (1707) by the Sphaerica of Theodosius, edited by Joseph Hunt, Master of Balliol College, followed in 1710 by Apollonius’s Conics with the lemmas of Pappus of Alexandria. To the same studies and vigils of Halley the Mathematical Republic owes this our Menelaus, which—though printed complete at the University Press under Halley’s own care—had not yet come forth into the light.

Book I — Definitions (Definitiones I–VI)

Definition I. A spherical triangle is the space comprehended under arcs of great circles upon the surface of a sphere.

Proposition I.1 — Problem: Constructing an Equal Spherical Angle

[§1.3 · Construction & Demonstration] Let the given point be \(\mathrm{B}\) on the given surface, and let the given arc of a great circle be arc \(\mathrm{AB}\), and let the given angle be \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma A E}\): and let it be required to construct at point \(\mathrm{B}\) an angle equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma A E}\).

Proposition I.4 — Theorem: SAS Spherical Congruence

Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) and \(\triangle \mathrm{\Delta EZ}\) be two spherical triangles, and let angle \(\angle \mathrm{B}\) of triangle \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) be equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{E}\) of triangle \(\triangle \mathrm{\Delta EZ}\), and arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}\) be equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}\), and arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{EZ}}\): I say that arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}\) is equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}\).

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

Proposition I.5 — Theorem: Spherical Triangle Inequality

Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) be a spherical triangle: I say that any two arcs of the three \(\mathrm{AB}, \mathrm{B\Gamma}, \mathrm{\Gamma A}\) comprehending it, taken together, are greater than the remaining arc.

Proposition I.8 — Theorem: Spherical Hinge & Greater Base

The proof of this is the same as in rectilinear triangles. Yet a demonstration of the same is also accomplished in another way. Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) and \(\triangle \mathrm{\Delta EZ}\) be two spherical triangles, and let arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}\) be equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}\), and arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{EZ}}\): I say that if angle \(\angle \mathrm{B}\) be greater than angle \(\angle \mathrm{E}\), arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}\) shall also be greater than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}\); and if arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}\) be greater than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}\), angle \(\angle \mathrm{B}\) shall also be greater than angle \(\angle \mathrm{E}\).

Proposition I.9 — Theorem: Greater Side Subtends Greater Angle

Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) be a spherical triangle, and let arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) be greater than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{BA}}\): I say that angle \(\angle \mathrm{A}\) is greater than angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma}\).

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

Proposition I.10 — Theorem: Semicircle Exterior-Angle Criterion

Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) be a spherical triangle, and let its two sides \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}, \widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) taken together be equal to a semicircle, and let arc \(\mathrm{A\Gamma}\) be produced: I say that the exterior angle \(\angle \mathrm{B\Gamma\Delta}\) is equal to the interior angle \(\angle \mathrm{A}\) opposite to it. And if the two arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}, \widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) be less than a semicircle, angle \(\angle \mathrm{B\Gamma\Delta}\) shall be greater than angle \(\angle \mathrm{A}\). But if arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}, \widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) be greater than a semicircle, angle \(\angle \mathrm{B\Gamma\Delta}\) shall be less than angle \(\angle \mathrm{A}\).

Proposition I.11 — Theorem: Spherical Angle-Sum Excess

[§1.4 · Demonstration] Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) be a spherical triangle: I say that the exterior angle contained by arcs \(\mathrm{B\Gamma}\) and \(\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}\) is less than angles \(\mathrm{A}\) and \(\mathrm{B}\) opposite to it; and that the three angles of the triangle \(\mathrm{A}, \mathrm{B}, \mathrm{\Gamma}\) taken together exceed two right angles.

English · Latin · Summary · Notes

Proposition I.17 — Theorem: AAA Spherical Congruence

[§1.5 · Menelaus’s Direct Intrinsic Demonstration] Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) and \(\triangle \mathrm{\Delta EZ}\) be two spherical triangles in which angle \(\angle \mathrm{A}\) is equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Delta}\), angle \(\angle \mathrm{B}\) to angle \(\angle \mathrm{E}\), and angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma}\) to angle \(\angle \mathrm{Z}\): I say that arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}\) is equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}\), arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{EZ}}\), and arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}\) to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}\).
