Propositions I.4 & I.5 — SAS Congruence & Spherical Triangle Inequality¶

Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. I Prop. IV & V (Halley 1758, pp. 5–6)

Proposition I.4 — Theorem: SAS Spherical Congruence¶

Enunciation (Halley 1758, p. 5)

If two spherical triangles have two sides equal to two sides respectively, each to each, and have the angles contained by those equal arcs equal to one another: they shall also have their bases equal to one another, and the remaining angles equal to the remaining angles, each to that which the equal arc subtends.

[Demonstration] Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) and \(\triangle \mathrm{\Delta EZ}\) be two spherical triangles having two sides \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}} = \widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}\) and \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}} = \widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}\), and angle \(\angle \mathrm{BA\Gamma} = \angle \mathrm{E\Delta Z}\): I say that base \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}} = \widehat{\mathrm{EZ}}\), angle \(\angle \mathrm{B} = \angle \mathrm{E}\), and angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma} = \angle \mathrm{Z}\).

Through the center of the sphere let straight radii be drawn to vertices \(\mathrm{A}, \mathrm{B}, \mathrm{\Gamma}\) and \(\mathrm{\Delta}, \mathrm{E}, \mathrm{Z}\). These straight lines form two trihedral solid angles at the center of the sphere. Because the plane angles subtended by the arcs are equal (\(\angle \mathrm{AOB} = \angle \mathrm{\Delta OE}\), \(\angle \mathrm{AO\Gamma} = \angle \mathrm{\Delta OZ}\)) and the dihedral inclinations of their containing planes are equal, the two trihedral solid angles are congruent (Euclid Elements XI.B / XI.10). Therefore the third plane angle \(\angle \mathrm{BO\Gamma}\) equals \(\angle \mathrm{EOZ}\), so base \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}} = \widehat{\mathrm{EZ}}\), and the remaining dihedral angles are respectively equal. Q.E.D.

Proposition I.5 — Theorem: Spherical Triangle Inequality¶

Enunciation (Halley 1758, p. 6)

In every spherical triangle, any two arcs taken together are greater than the remaining arc.

[Demonstration] In spherical triangle \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\), I say that any two sides taken together are greater than the remaining side: \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}} + \widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}} > \widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}\).

From the center of the sphere \(\mathrm{O}\) draw radii \(\mathrm{OA}, \mathrm{OB}, \mathrm{O\Gamma}\). These three radii form a trihedral angle with vertex \(\mathrm{O}\) bounded by the three plane angles \(\angle \mathrm{AOB}, \angle \mathrm{BO\Gamma}, \angle \mathrm{AO\Gamma}\). By Euclid Elements XI.20 (If a solid angle be contained by three plane angles, any two, taken together in any manner, are greater than the remaining one), any two of the plane angles taken together are greater than the third:

\[\angle \mathrm{AOB} + \angle \mathrm{BO\Gamma} \;>\; \angle \mathrm{AO\Gamma}.\]

Since on a sphere equal plane angles at the center subtend equal great-circle arcs, and greater plane angles subtend greater arcs, it immediately follows that:

\[\widehat{\mathrm{AB}} + \widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}} \;>\; \widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}.\]

Quod erat demonstrandum.
