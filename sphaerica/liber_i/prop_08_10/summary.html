Analytical Summary: SSS & the Semicircle Curvature Threshold¶

Deductive Significance of Proposition I.10

  1. Euclidean Exterior Angle Failure (Elements I.16): In Euclidean planar geometry, the exterior angle is unconditionally strictly greater than either remote interior angle (Elements I.16: \(\angle_{\text{ext}} > \angle_{\text{int}}\)). On the sphere, this fundamental theorem of neutral geometry fails because two great circles intersect twice at antipodal points, forming a spherical lune whose sides are semicircles with equal antipodal angles!

  2. The Semicircle Sum Condition (Proposition I.10): Menelaus establishes the precise curvature-dependent criterion: - When \(a + b < \pi\), the Euclidean inequality holds: \(\angle_{\text{ext}} > \angle_{\text{int}}\). - When \(a + b = \pi\), equality holds: \(\angle_{\text{ext}} = \angle_{\text{int}}\). - When \(a + b > \pi\), the inequality reverses: \(\angle_{\text{ext}} < \angle_{\text{int}}\)!

  3. Engine of Book I: Proposition I.10 is the universal tool that unlocks both Spherical Angle-Sum Excess (Proposition I.11) and AAA Congruence (Proposition I.17).
