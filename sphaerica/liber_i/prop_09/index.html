Proposition I.9 — Theorem: Greater Side Subtends Greater Angle¶

Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. I Prop. IX. Theor. (Halley 1758, p. 9)

Enunciation (Halley 1758, p. 9)

In every spherical triangle, the greater arc subtends the greater angle.

Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) be a spherical triangle, and let arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) be greater than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{BA}}\): I say that angle \(\angle \mathrm{A}\) is greater than angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma}\).

On arc \(\mathrm{B\Gamma}\) let \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}\) be made equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}\), and through points \(\mathrm{A}, \mathrm{\Delta}\) let great-circle arc \(\mathrm{A\Delta}\) be drawn. Since therefore the two arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}, \widehat{\mathrm{B\Delta}}\) taken together (by Proposition I.5) are greater than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Delta}}\), and arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}\) is equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta\Gamma}}\), arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Delta\Gamma}}\) will be greater than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Delta}}\). And arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta\Gamma}}\) is equal to \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}\), and \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}\) is common; hence the two arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta\Gamma}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma A}}\) are equal to the two \(\widehat{\mathrm{BA}}, \widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}\), each to its corresponding arc. And base \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Delta\Gamma}}\) is greater than base \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Delta}}\): wherefore (by Proposition I.8) angle \(\angle \mathrm{BA\Gamma}\) is greater than angle \(\angle \mathrm{A\Gamma B}\). Quod erat demonstrandum.
