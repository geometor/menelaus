Proposition I.4 — Theorem: SAS Spherical Congruence¶

Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. I Prop. IV. Theor. (Halley 1758, pp. 5–6)

Enunciation (Halley 1758, p. 5)

If in two spherical triangles some angle of the one be equal to an angle of the other, and the arcs containing those equal angles be equal, each to each; the two remaining arcs shall be equal. And if the two remaining arcs be equal, the angles contained by the equal arcs in either triangle shall be equal.

Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) and \(\triangle \mathrm{\Delta EZ}\) be two spherical triangles, and let angle \(\angle \mathrm{B}\) of triangle \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) be equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{E}\) of triangle \(\triangle \mathrm{\Delta EZ}\), and arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}\) be equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}\), and arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{EZ}}\): I say that arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}\) is equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}\).

With pole \(\mathrm{B}\) and radius \(\mathrm{BA}\) describe arc \(\mathrm{AH}\); and with pole \(\mathrm{E}\) and radius \(\mathrm{E\Delta}\) describe arc \(\mathrm{\Delta\Theta}\). Because angle \(\angle \mathrm{B}\) is equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{E}\), and arc \(\mathrm{AH}\) is described with pole \(\mathrm{B}\) and the same radius with which arc \(\mathrm{\Delta\Theta}\) is described with pole \(\mathrm{E}\), arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AH}}\) will be equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta\Theta}}\) (by Coroll. 1 of Prop. I.1). And since arc \(\mathrm{B\Gamma}\) passes through the pole of arc \(\mathrm{AH}\), it will be perpendicular to it; and in like manner arc \(\mathrm{EZ}\) will be perpendicular to arc \(\mathrm{\Delta\Theta}\): and because arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) is equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{EZ}}\), and \(\widehat{\mathrm{BH}}\) to \(\widehat{\mathrm{E\Theta}}\) (for each is equal to \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}\)), the remaining arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{H\Gamma}}\) will be equal to the remaining arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta Z}}\). Now since upon the diameters of two equal circles, of which \(\mathrm{AH}\), \(\mathrm{\Delta\Theta}\) are arcs, equal segments of two equal circles stand at right angles—namely arcs \(\mathrm{\Theta ZE}\), \(\mathrm{H\Gamma B}\) continued—

and in both segments equal portions less than their halves are taken, namely arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta Z}}\), \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma H}}\); and in equal circles equal segments \(\widehat{\mathrm{AH}}\), \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta\Delta}}\) are held: the straight line joining points \(\mathrm{Z}\), \(\mathrm{\Delta}\) will be equal (by Theodosius II.12) to the straight line joining points \(\mathrm{\Gamma}\), \(\mathrm{A}\); and therefore arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Delta}}\) is equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma A}}\).

And if arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma A}}\) be equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Delta}}\), angle \(\angle \mathrm{B}\) will be equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{E}\).

The demonstration of this is the converse of the preceding. For if arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Delta}}\) be equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma A}}\), the straight line joining points \(\mathrm{Z}\), \(\mathrm{\Delta}\) will be equal to the straight line joining \(\mathrm{\Gamma}\), \(\mathrm{A}\). But arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta Z}}\) is equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma H}}\), each of which stands at right angles upon the diameter of the circle on which it rests; and those circles are equal: wherefore (by Theodosius II.11) arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta\Delta}}\) is equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{AH}}\), and consequently (by Coroll. 1 of Prop. I.1) angle \(\angle \mathrm{E}\) is equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{B}\). Quod erat demonstrandum.
