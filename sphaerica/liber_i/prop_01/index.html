Proposition I.1 — Problem: Constructing an Equal Spherical Angle¶

Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. I Prop. I (Halley 1758, p. 2)

Enunciation (Halley 1758, p. 2)

At a given point on a given arc of a great circle on the surface of a sphere, to construct a spherical angle equal to a given spherical angle contained by two arcs of great circles on that surface.

[§1.3 · Construction & Demonstration] Let the given point be \(\mathrm{B}\) on the given surface, and let the given arc of a great circle be arc \(\mathrm{AB}\), and let the given angle be \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma A E}\): and let it be required to construct at point \(\mathrm{B}\) an angle equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma A E}\).

Describe with pole \(\mathrm{\Delta}\) and with any radius the arc \(\mathrm{E\Gamma}\), and with pole also \(\mathrm{B}\) and with the same radius describe arc \(\mathrm{AZ}\), and let arc \(\mathrm{AZ}\) be taken equal to arc \(\mathrm{E\Gamma}\), and through the two points \(\mathrm{B}, \mathrm{Z}\) let the arc of a great circle \(\mathrm{BZ}\) pass: I say that angle \(\angle \mathrm{ABZ}\) is equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma AE}\).

For since the two arcs \(\mathrm{\Gamma A}, \mathrm{\Delta E}\) are arcs of great circles passing through the poles of circle \(\mathrm{\Gamma E}\), the plane of each will cut the circumference of circle \(\mathrm{\Gamma E}\) in two equal parts and at right angles; therefore both of the common sections of the planes of arcs \(\mathrm{\Gamma A}, \mathrm{\Delta E}\) with the plane of arc \(\mathrm{\Gamma E}\) will pass through the center of the circle whose arc is \(\mathrm{\Gamma E}\); and the common intersection of the planes of arcs \(\mathrm{\Gamma A}, \mathrm{\Delta E}\) will be perpendicular to the plane of the circle whose arc is \(\mathrm{\Gamma E}\), and to any straight line drawn from the center of circle \(\mathrm{\Gamma E}\): so that each of the straight lines drawn from points \(\mathrm{\Gamma}, \mathrm{E}\) to the center will be perpendicular to the common section of the planes \(\mathrm{\Gamma A}, \mathrm{\Delta E}\).

In like manner it will be established that the straight lines proceeding from points \(\mathrm{A}, \mathrm{Z}\) to the center of circle \(\mathrm{AZ}\) are perpendicular to the section of the planes \(\mathrm{AB}, \mathrm{BZ}\). And since arc \(\mathrm{\Gamma E}\) was described with pole \(\mathrm{\Delta}\), and with an equal radius arc \(\mathrm{AZ}\) was described with pole \(\mathrm{B}\), the circle whose arc is \(\mathrm{\Gamma E}\) will be equal to the circle whose arc is \(\mathrm{AZ}\). And arc \(\mathrm{\Gamma E}\) is equal to arc \(\mathrm{AZ}\): wherefore the angle which arc \(\mathrm{\Gamma E}\) subtends at its center is equal to the angle which arc \(\mathrm{AZ}\) subtends at its center.

Therefore the angle contained by the two perpendiculars from the common section is equal to the angle contained by the other two perpendiculars from the other common section; and consequently the dihedral inclination of the planes \(\mathrm{AB}, \mathrm{BZ}\) equals the dihedral inclination of the planes \(\mathrm{\Gamma A}, \mathrm{\Delta E}\). Hence spherical angle \(\angle \mathrm{ABZ}\) is equal to spherical angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Gamma AE}\). Quod erat faciendum.
