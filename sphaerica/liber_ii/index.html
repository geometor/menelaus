Book II: Spherical Arcs, Great Circles & Rising-Time Inequalities¶

Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Liber Secundus — Edmond Halley (1758, pp. 48–79)

Where Book I established the pure intrinsic geometry of an isolated spherical triangle, Book II (Propositions II.1, II.10–II.13, Halley pp. 48–79) applies those triangle inequalities to families of great circles cutting across an oblique great circle (such as the ecliptic) and systems of parallel circles (diurnal parallels to the celestial equator).

On page 75, before Proposition II.10, Menelaus delivers his famous programmatic critique of his predecessor Theodosius of Bithynia:

“Having therefore premised these demonstrations, we have subjoined what follows in order to verify the propositions contained in the book of Theodosius On the Sphere, together with their converses—yet in a manner more universal and such as compels assent, both for establishing the first principles and for demonstrating the things written previously, some of which indeed scarcely appear sufficiently firm.”

—Menelaus of Alexandria, Sphaerica Book II (Halley 1758, p. 75)

Book II systematically collapses multiple tedious 3D chord cases into elegant, universal synthetic theorems derived from the intrinsic geometry of spherical triangles on \(S^2\).

Sections & Propositions¶

Proposition II.1 — Problem: Oblique Spherical Angle Construction

[§2.1 · Construction & Demonstration, Halley pp. 48–49] Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) be a spherical triangle such as we have described, so that \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}, \widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) are less than a semicircle; and let point \(\mathrm{\Delta}\) be taken on arc \(\mathrm{B\Gamma}\): it is required to draw from point \(\mathrm{\Delta}\) to arc \(\mathrm{A\Gamma}\) an arc of a great circle containing with \(\mathrm{A\Gamma}\) itself an angle equal to the obtuse angle \(\angle \mathrm{BA\Gamma}\).

Proposition II.11 — Theorem: Symmetry of Polar Interceptions

Let \(\mathrm{ABE}\), \(\mathrm{H\Theta\Lambda}\) be two great circles of the sphere intersecting one another at point \(\mathrm{\Gamma}\), and on one of them let arcs \(\mathrm{AB}\), \(\mathrm{\Delta E}\) be taken equidistant from point \(\mathrm{\Gamma}\); and let great-circle arcs be drawn passing through points \(\mathrm{A}, \mathrm{B}, \mathrm{\Delta}, \mathrm{E}\) and through \(\mathrm{Z}\), the pole of either of the circles \(\mathrm{ABE}\), \(\mathrm{H\Gamma\Lambda}\); let these arcs be \(\mathrm{ZAH}\), \(\mathrm{ZB\Theta}\), \(\mathrm{ZK\Delta}\), \(\mathrm{Z\Lambda E}\): I say that arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{H\Theta}}\) is equal to arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{K\Lambda}}\).

Proposition II.13 — Theorem: Oblique Arcs Touching a Larger Parallel

Let one of the parallel circles, namely \(\mathrm{A\Delta E}\), touch great circle \(\mathrm{AB}\); and let the greatest of the parallels be \(\mathrm{B\Xi X}\); and on arc \(\mathrm{AB}\) let two equal arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta K}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda M}}\) be taken; through whose endpoints let parallel circles \(\mathrm{K\Sigma}, \mathrm{\Lambda O}, \mathrm{M\Pi}\), and other great circles \(\mathrm{\Theta X}, \mathrm{KP}, \mathrm{\Lambda\Xi}, \mathrm{MT}\), be described, all of which touch one and the same parallel circle larger than circle \(\mathrm{A\Delta E}\): I say that arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Pi O}}\) is less than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Sigma\Theta}}\), and that arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{T\Xi}}\) is less than \(\widehat{\mathrm{XP}}\).
