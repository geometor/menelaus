Proposition II.10 — Universal Theorem Superseding Theodosius III¶

Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. II Prop. X & Censura Theodosii (Halley 1758, pp. 75–76)

Menelaus’s Programmatic Critique of Theodosius (p. 75)¶

“Having therefore premised these demonstrations, we have subjoined what follows in order to verify the propositions contained in the book of Theodosius On the Sphere, together with their converses—yet in a manner more universal and such as compels assent, both for establishing the first principles and for demonstrating the things written previously, some of which indeed scarcely appear sufficiently firm.”

—Menelaus of Alexandria, Sphaerica Book II (Halley 1758, p. 75)

Proposition II.10 — Universal Rising-Time Theorem¶

Enunciation (Halley 1758, p. 75)

If on a sphere there be a great circle touched by one of the parallel circles, and on that great circle two equal arcs be taken between the point of contact and the greatest of the parallel circles; and through the endpoints of those arcs there be described both parallel circles and great circles issuing from the pole: then those parallel circles shall cut off unequal arcs from the great circles drawn from the pole, of which the arc nearer to the greatest of the parallels shall be greater than the more remote. Moreover, that portion of the greatest of the parallels cut off by the great circles drawn from the pole which lies nearer to the common intersection of the oblique circle and the greatest parallel shall be less than the portion more remote from the same.

[§2.3 · Demonstration & Note, pp. 75–76] Let \(\mathrm{\Theta MB}\) be a great circle of the sphere, touched by one of the parallel circles \(\mathrm{A\Delta E}\), and let the greatest of the parallel circles be \(\mathrm{BT\Xi PX}\); and on \(\mathrm{\Theta B}\) let two equal arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta K}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda M}}\) be taken; and through the pole \(\mathrm{\Gamma}\) and the points \(\mathrm{\Theta}, \mathrm{K}, \mathrm{\Lambda}, \mathrm{M}\) let great-circle arcs \(\mathrm{\Gamma\Theta X}\), \(\mathrm{\Gamma KP}\), \(\mathrm{\Gamma\Lambda\Xi}\), \(\mathrm{\Gamma MT}\) be drawn; and let parallel circles pass through points \(\mathrm{K}, \mathrm{\Lambda}, \mathrm{M}\), namely arcs \(\mathrm{K\Sigma}\), \(\mathrm{\Lambda O}\), \(\mathrm{M\Pi}\):

f. 0092 · p. 76

I say that arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Pi O}}\) is greater than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Sigma\Theta}}\), and that arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{XP}}\) is greater than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Xi T}}\).

For since \(\triangle \mathrm{M\Gamma\Theta}\) is a spherical triangle, whose leg \(\widehat{\mathrm{M\Gamma}}\) is greater than leg \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Theta}}\), and each is less than a quadrant; and on \(\mathrm{\Theta M}\) equal arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta K}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda M}}\) are taken: it follows of necessity—by what we demonstrated in Book I Prop. 33 and Book II Prop. 7—that angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Theta\Gamma K}\) is greater than angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Lambda\Gamma M}\), and arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{XP}}\) is greater than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Xi T}}\); and that arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{M\Gamma}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Theta}}\) taken together exceed \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda\Gamma}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma K}}\) taken together; whence it is manifest that arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Pi O}}\) is greater than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Sigma\Theta}}\).

  • N.B. (p. 76): “Observe that this proposition comprises the 5th, 6th, and 9th of Book III of Theodosius’s Sphaerica.”
