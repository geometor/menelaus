Proposition II.12 — Theorem: Oblique Arcs Touching a Smaller Parallel¶

Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. II Prop. XII (Halley 1758, pp. 77–78)

Enunciation (Halley 1758, p. 77)

If there be a great circle on a sphere touched by one of the parallel circles, and between the point of contact and the greatest of the parallels two equal arcs be taken; and arcs of circles be drawn passing through the endpoints of the arcs taken, of which some are arcs of parallel circles, and others are arcs of great circles either passing through the poles of the parallels, or touching one and the same parallel circle yet smaller than the prior parallel, and inclining toward the same parts upon the greatest of the parallels toward which the aforesaid oblique circle inclines: then the parallel circles so drawn shall cut off unequal arcs from the great circles drawn, of which that nearer to the greatest of the parallels shall be greater than the more remote. Moreover, the portions cut off on the greatest of the parallels which are nearer to the common intersection of that circle with the primary oblique circle shall be less than those more remote from the same.

Let \(\mathrm{AMB}\) be a great circle of the sphere touched by one of the parallel circles \(\mathrm{A\Delta E}\), and let the greatest of the parallels be \(\mathrm{\Gamma XP\Xi TB}\); and on circle \(\mathrm{AMB}\) let two equal arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta K}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda M}}\) be taken; and let arcs of circles be described passing through their endpoints: of the parallels, arcs \(\mathrm{K\Sigma}, \mathrm{\Lambda O}, \mathrm{M\Pi}\); and of great circles, \(\mathrm{KP}, \mathrm{\Lambda\Xi}, \mathrm{MT}\), either passing through their poles, or all of them

touching the same parallel circle smaller than circle \(\mathrm{A\Delta E}\), and inclined toward the same side toward which circle \(\mathrm{AMB}\) is inclined upon circle \(\mathrm{\Gamma B}\): I say that \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Pi O}}\) is greater than \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta\Sigma}}\), and \(\widehat{\mathrm{XP}}\) than \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Xi T}}\).

Because angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Theta B\Gamma}\) is less than a right angle, while angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Theta XB}\) is not less than a right angle, arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta X}}\) will be less than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta B}}\). Since therefore triangle \(\triangle \mathrm{B\Theta X}\) has leg \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Theta}}\) greater than leg \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta X}}\), and \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Theta}}\) is not greater than a quadrant of a circle, nor angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Theta}\) greater than a right angle, and from arc \(\mathrm{B\Theta}\) two equal arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta K}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda M}}\) are cut off, and arcs \(\mathrm{KP}, \mathrm{\Lambda\Xi}, \mathrm{MT}\) are drawn making with base \(\mathrm{B\Gamma}\) angles equal to angle \(\angle \mathrm{\Theta XB}\) relative to them: arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{XP}}\) (by Book II Prop. 7) will be greater than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Xi T}}\), and the two arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta X}}, \widehat{\mathrm{MT}}\) taken together will be less than the two arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{KP}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda\Xi}}\) taken together; that is, arcs \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta X}}, \widehat{\mathrm{X\Pi}}\) taken together will be less than \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Sigma X}}, \widehat{\mathrm{XO}}\) taken together. Hence it will be established that \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Pi O}}\) is greater than arc \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta\Sigma}}\). And angle \(\angle \mathrm{B\Theta X}\) will not be greater than a right angle, as was said, because in triangle \(\triangle \mathrm{BX\Theta}\) the angle at \(\mathrm{X}\) is not less than a right angle, while the sides containing the other angle, namely the angle at \(\mathrm{\Theta}\), are each less than a quadrant of a circle. Now it was demonstrated in Book I Proposition XXI that if these things be so, the contained angle \(\angle \mathrm{B\Theta X}\) will not be greater than a right angle. Quod erat demonstrandum.
