Proposition II.1 — Problem: Oblique Spherical Angle Construction¶

Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. II Prop. I (Halley 1758, p. 48)

Enunciation (Halley 1758, p. 48)

From a point taken on the side of an obtuse-angled spherical triangle opposite to the obtuse angle, to draw an arc of a great circle which, with one of the arcs containing the obtuse angle, shall comprehend an angle equal to that same obtuse angle.

[§2.1 · Construction & Demonstration, Halley p. 48] Let \(\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}\) be an obtuse-angled spherical triangle as described, such that \(\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}\) and \(\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}\) are each less than a semicircle, with the obtuse angle at \(\mathrm{A}\) (\(\angle \mathrm{BA\Gamma}\)); and let a point \(\mathrm{\Delta}\) be taken on arc \(\mathrm{B\Gamma}\). It is required to draw from point \(\mathrm{\Delta}\) to arc \(\mathrm{A\Gamma}\) an arc of a great circle comprehending with \(\mathrm{A\Gamma}\) an angle equal to the obtuse angle \(\angle \mathrm{BA\Gamma}\).

Let \(\mathrm{M}\) be the pole of arc \(\mathrm{A\Gamma}\), and through points \(\mathrm{M}, \mathrm{\Delta}\) let the great-circle arc \(\mathrm{M\Delta I}\) be drawn (meeting \(\mathrm{A\Gamma}\) orthogonally at \(\mathrm{I}\)); produce arc \(\mathrm{A\Gamma}\) to \(\mathrm{E}\) so that \(\widehat{\mathrm{AE}}\) and \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma I}}\) are equal, and draw the great-circle arc \(\mathrm{ME}\).

Because \(\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma B}} + \widehat{\mathrm{BA}}\) taken together are less than a semicircle, Menelaus applies Book I Proposition I.13 (and subsequent cevian separation lemmas in Props. II.2–II.9) to locate the unique transversal arc through \(\mathrm{\Delta}\) reproducing angle \(\angle \mathrm{BA\Gamma}\). Quod erat faciendum.
