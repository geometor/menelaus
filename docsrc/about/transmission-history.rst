Textual Transmission: Greek, Arabic, Hebrew, Maurolico (1558), Halley (1758) & Björnbo (1902)
=============================================================================================

Loss of the Greek Archetype & Survival of Greek Witnesses
---------------------------------------------------------

Although the continuous three-book Greek manuscript of :person:`Menelaus <menelaus>`'s *Sphaerica* perished in late antiquity, two decisive polytonic Greek witnesses survive in public-domain critical editions:

1. **Ptolemy's *Syntaxis Mathematica* (*Almagest*) I.13** (:cite:`ed. Heiberg, 1898, pp. 68–76 <ptolemy-almagest-i-13>`): Under the heading *Προλαμβανόμενα εἰς τὰς σφαιρικὰς δείξεις* ("Preliminaries to Spherical Demonstrations"), :person:`Claudius Ptolemy <ptolemy>` proves the Planar Menelaus Lemma (*κατὰ σύνθεσιν* and *κατὰ διαίρεσιν*), two chord-division lemmas, and the Spherical Menelaus Theorem in :term:`chords of double arcs <subtensa-dupli-arcus>` (*ἡ ὑπὸ τὴν διπλῆν τῆς περιφερείας*).
2. **Pappus's *Synagoge* Book VI & Proclus on Euclid I**: :person:`Pappus <pappus>` (:cite:`ed. Hultsch, 1877 <pappus-hultsch-1877>`) cites Menelaus's term *τρίπλευρον* ("trilateral") for the spherical triangle and preserves propositions from Book II on rising times of ecliptic arcs.

Arabic, Latin & Hebrew Medieval Stemmata
----------------------------------------

As :person:`George Costard <costard>` recounts in his 1758 Oxford *Praefatio* (:cite:`pp. iii–v <costard-1758>`) and :person:`Axel Anthon Björnbo <bjornbo>` reconstructs in :cite:`Studien über Menelaos' Sphärik (1902) <bjornbo-1902>`:

- **Baghdad & Khwarazm (9th–11th c.)**: Translated into Arabic by :person:`Isḥāq ibn Ḥunayn <ishaq-ibn-hunayn>` (*c.* 890 CE), commented upon by :person:`Thābit ibn Qurra <thabit-ibn-qurra>` in his *Treatise on the Sector Figure* (:term:`al-Shakl al-Qaṭṭāʿ <figura-catha>`), and critically redacted in 1007–1008 CE by :person:`Abū Naṣr Manṣūr <abu-nasr-mansur>`, who rendered the compound proportions cleanly in terms of :term:`sines <sinus>` (*jayb*).
- **Toledo & Montpellier (12th–13th c.)**: :person:`Gerard of Cremona <gerard-of-cremona>` translated an Arabic redaction into Latin at Toledo, while :person:`Jacob ben Machir ibn Tibbon <jacob-ben-machir>` (*Prophatius*) translated Isḥāq's lineage into Hebrew—preserved in the Bodleian Huntington manuscripts at Oxford using *nekhohot* (נכחות, *subtensa dupli arcus*, Halley 1758 p. 82).

From Maurolico (1558) to Halley's Oxford Edition (1758)
-------------------------------------------------------

- **Francesco Maurolico (Messina, 1558)**: In :cite:`Menelai Sphaericorum Lib. III <maurolico-1558>`, :person:`Maurolico <maurolico>` hailed Menelaus's treatise as the source "*whence the whole science of spherical triangles flows*" (*unde tota sphaeralium Triangulorum Scientia scaturit*), yet freely interpolated his own lemmas and reorganized Book II–III; :person:`Marin Mersenne <mersenne>` condensed Maurolico's text in :cite:`1644 <mersenne-1644>`.
- **Edmond Halley (Oxford, 1758)**: Savilian Professor :person:`Edmond Halley <halley>` collated the Bodleian Hebrew Huntington codex directly against Arabic manuscripts, stripped away Renaissance interpolations, engraved accurate geometric woodcuts with Greek capital labels (``Α, Β, Γ, Δ, Ε, Ζ, Η, Θ, Κ, Λ, Μ, Ν``), and added incisive philological scholia (notably the famous p. 82 *Scholion* on *subtensa dupli arcus* vs. *Sinus* and *Figura Catha*). Issued posthumously in 1758 with :person:`Costard <costard>`'s preface, Halley's Latin text—paired with :person:`Heiberg <heiberg>`'s 1898 Greek *Almagest* text and :person:`Björnbo <bjornbo>`'s 1902 Teubner analysis—forms the 100% public-domain foundation of this Digital Scriptorium Codex.
