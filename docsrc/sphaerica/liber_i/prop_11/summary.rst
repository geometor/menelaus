Analytical Summary: Spherical Angle-Sum Excess
==============================================

.. rubric:: *Deductive Significance of Proposition I.11*

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0027
   :page: 11

1. **Overturning Euclid Elements I.32**:
   In Euclidean plane geometry, the sum of the three angles of any triangle equals exactly two right angles (:math:`A + B + C = \pi`). Menelaus proves the foundational theorem of elliptic/spherical non-Euclidean geometry: on a sphere, the interior angle sum strictly exceeds two right angles (:math:`A + B + C > \pi`).

2. **The Antipodal Lune Construction**:
   - Menelaus produces sides :math:`AB` and :math:`A\Gamma` until they intersect at the antipodal point :math:`\Delta` of vertex :math:`A`.
   - The two great-circle arcs :math:`AB\Delta` and :math:`A\Gamma\Delta` form a spherical lune of two full semicircles.
   - By constructing an angle :math:`\angle \Delta\Gamma E = \angle A` at :math:`\Gamma`, he creates an isosceles spherical triangle :math:`\triangle \Delta E\Gamma` whose leg sum with :math:`BE` is less than a semicircle (:math:`BE + E\Gamma = B\Delta < \pi`).
   - Applying Proposition I.10 to :math:`\triangle B\Gamma E` yields the inequality :math:`\angle_{\text{ext}} < \angle A + \angle B`, and adding the supplementary angle immediately proves :math:`A + B + C > \pi`.

3. **Spherical Excess (:term:`excessus-sphaericus`)**:
   The difference :math:`E = A + B + C - \pi > 0` is the *spherical excess*, later shown by Thomas Harriot (1603) and Albert Girard (1629) to be strictly proportional to the surface area of the spherical triangle: :math:`\text{Area}(\triangle ABC) = R^2 E`.
