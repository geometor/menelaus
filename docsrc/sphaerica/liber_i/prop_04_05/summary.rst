Analytical Summary: SAS Congruence & the Triangle Inequality
============================================================

.. rubric:: *Deductive Architecture of Propositions I.4 and I.5*

1. **SAS Congruence Without Planar Superposition (Proposition I.4)**:
   In planar geometry, Euclid *Elements* I.4 relied on rigid translation and superposition of one triangle upon another. On a sphere, Menelaus establishes SAS by considering the two trihedral solid angles formed at the center of the sphere by radii drawn to the vertices. Because two face angles and the intervening dihedral angle are equal, the trihedra are congruent, forcing base equality and angle equality directly on the surface.

2. **The Spherical Triangle Inequality (Proposition I.5)**:
   Euclid *Elements* I.20 proves :math:`a + b > c` in the plane by producing side :math:`AB` to :math:`D` with :math:`BD = BC` and applying isosceles angle comparisons. On the sphere, Menelaus deduces the triangle inequality directly from the solid-angle theorem of Euclid *Elements* XI.20: the sum of two face angles of a trihedral angle exceeds the third.
