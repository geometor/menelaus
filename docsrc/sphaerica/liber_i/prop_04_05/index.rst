Propositions I.4 & I.5 — SAS Congruence & Spherical Triangle Inequality
========================================================================

.. rubric:: *Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. I Prop. IV & V (Halley 1758, pp. 5–6)*

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0021
   :page: 5

Proposition I.4 — Theorem: SAS Spherical Congruence
---------------------------------------------------

.. admonition:: Enunciation (*Halley 1758, p. 5*)

   *If two spherical triangles have two sides equal to two sides respectively, each to each, and have the angles contained by those equal arcs equal to one another: they shall also have their bases equal to one another, and the remaining angles equal to the remaining angles, each to that which the equal arc subtends.*

**[Demonstration]** Let :math:`\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}` and :math:`\triangle \mathrm{\Delta EZ}` be two spherical triangles having two sides :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{AB}} = \widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}` and :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}} = \widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}`, and angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{BA\Gamma} = \angle \mathrm{E\Delta Z}`: I say that base :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}} = \widehat{\mathrm{EZ}}`, angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{B} = \angle \mathrm{E}`, and angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Gamma} = \angle \mathrm{Z}`.

Through the center of the sphere let straight radii be drawn to vertices :math:`\mathrm{A}, \mathrm{B}, \mathrm{\Gamma}` and :math:`\mathrm{\Delta}, \mathrm{E}, \mathrm{Z}`. These straight lines form two trihedral solid angles at the center of the sphere. Because the plane angles subtended by the arcs are equal (:math:`\angle \mathrm{AOB} = \angle \mathrm{\Delta OE}`, :math:`\angle \mathrm{AO\Gamma} = \angle \mathrm{\Delta OZ}`) and the dihedral inclinations of their containing planes are equal, the two trihedral solid angles are congruent (Euclid *Elements* XI.B / XI.10). Therefore the third plane angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{BO\Gamma}` equals :math:`\angle \mathrm{EOZ}`, so base :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}} = \widehat{\mathrm{EZ}}`, and the remaining dihedral angles are respectively equal. *Q.E.D.*

----

Proposition I.5 — Theorem: Spherical Triangle Inequality
--------------------------------------------------------

.. admonition:: Enunciation (*Halley 1758, p. 6*)

   *In every spherical triangle, any two arcs taken together are greater than the remaining arc.*

**[Demonstration]** In spherical triangle :math:`\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}`, I say that any two sides taken together are greater than the remaining side: :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{AB}} + \widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}} > \widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}`.

From the center of the sphere :math:`\mathrm{O}` draw radii :math:`\mathrm{OA}, \mathrm{OB}, \mathrm{O\Gamma}`. These three radii form a trihedral angle with vertex :math:`\mathrm{O}` bounded by the three plane angles :math:`\angle \mathrm{AOB}, \angle \mathrm{BO\Gamma}, \angle \mathrm{AO\Gamma}`. By Euclid *Elements* XI.20 (*If a solid angle be contained by three plane angles, any two, taken together in any manner, are greater than the remaining one*), any two of the plane angles taken together are greater than the third:

.. math::

   \angle \mathrm{AOB} + \angle \mathrm{BO\Gamma} \;>\; \angle \mathrm{AO\Gamma}.

Since on a sphere equal plane angles at the center subtend equal great-circle arcs, and greater plane angles subtend greater arcs, it immediately follows that:

.. math::

   \widehat{\mathrm{AB}} + \widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}} \;>\; \widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}.

*Quod erat demonstrandum.*
