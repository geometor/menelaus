Proposition I.8 — Theorem: Spherical Hinge & Greater Base
=========================================================

.. rubric:: *Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. I Prop. VIII. Theor. (Halley 1758, pp. 8–9)*

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0024
   :page: 8

.. picture:: 0024.g1.svg
   :alt: Calibrated vector diagram for folio 0024.g1

.. picture:: 0024.g1.png
   :alt: Archival scan of folio 0024.g1

.. admonition:: Enunciation (*Halley 1758, p. 8*)

   *If two spherical triangles have two sides equal to two sides respectively, each to each; and the angle of the one contained by those equal sides be greater than the angle of the other: the arc subtended by the greater angle shall be greater than the arc subtended by the lesser; and if the arc be greater, the angle shall also be greater.*

The proof of this is the same as in rectilinear triangles. Yet a demonstration of the same is also accomplished in another way. Let :math:`\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}` and :math:`\triangle \mathrm{\Delta EZ}` be two spherical triangles, and let arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}` be equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}`, and arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}` to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{EZ}}`: I say that if angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{B}` be greater than angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{E}`, arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}` shall also be greater than arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}`; and if arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}` be greater than arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}`, angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{B}` shall also be greater than angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{E}`.

With poles :math:`\mathrm{B}, \mathrm{E}` and equal radii :math:`\mathrm{AB}, \mathrm{\Delta E}` describe arcs :math:`\mathrm{AH}, \mathrm{\Delta\Theta}`; and since arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{EZ}}` is equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}`, and arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{BH}}` to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{E\Theta}}`, the remaining arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{H\Gamma}}` will be equal to the remaining arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta Z}}`. Now because equal segments of equal circles begun from points :math:`\mathrm{H}, \mathrm{\Theta}`—namely the continued segments :math:`\mathrm{H\Gamma}, \mathrm{\Theta Z}`—stand at right angles upon the diameters of the equal circles whose arcs are :math:`\mathrm{AH}, \mathrm{\Delta\Theta}`, and in both

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0025
   :page: 9

segments equal arcs less than their halves are taken, namely arcs :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma H}}, \widehat{\mathrm{Z\Theta}}`; and because angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{B}` is greater than angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{E}`, arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta\Theta}}` is less than arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{AH}}`: therefore (*by Theodosius III.1*) the straight line joining points :math:`\mathrm{Z}, \mathrm{\Delta}` will be less than the join :math:`\mathrm{A\Gamma}`, and consequently arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}` is greater than arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}`.

And if arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}` be greater than arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}`, by the like argument it will be proved that the straight line joining :math:`\mathrm{Z}, \mathrm{\Delta}` is less than the line joining points :math:`\mathrm{A}, \mathrm{\Gamma}`; whence arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta\Delta}}` will also be less than arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{AH}}`, and therefore angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{B}` is greater than angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{E}`. *Quod erat demonstrandum.*
