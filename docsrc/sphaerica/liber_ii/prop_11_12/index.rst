Propositions II.11–II.12 — Symmetrical Equatorial & Oblique Horizon Arcs
========================================================================

.. rubric:: *Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. II Props. XI & XII (Halley 1758, pp. 76–79)*

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0092
   :page: 76

Proposition II.11 — Symmetry of Polar Interceptions (p. 76)
-----------------------------------------------------------

.. admonition:: Enunciation (*Halley 1758, p. 76*)

   *If two great circles on a sphere intersect one another, and on one of them two equal arcs be taken equidistant from their point of intersection; and through the endpoints of these arcs and through the poles of either great circle there pass great circles: those circles shall cut off equal arcs from the other of the first great circles.*

**[§2.4 · Demonstration, Halley p. 76]** Let :math:`\mathrm{ABE}`, :math:`\mathrm{H\Theta\Lambda}` be two great circles of the sphere intersecting at point :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma}`, and on one of them let arcs :math:`\mathrm{AB}`, :math:`\mathrm{\Delta E}` be taken equidistant from point :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma}`; and let great-circle arcs passing through points :math:`\mathrm{A}, \mathrm{B}, \mathrm{\Delta}, \mathrm{E}` and through the :term:`pole <polus>` :math:`\mathrm{Z}` of either circle be drawn: namely, arcs :math:`\mathrm{ZAH}`, :math:`\mathrm{ZB\Theta}`, :math:`\mathrm{ZK\Delta}`, :math:`\mathrm{Z\Lambda E}`. I say that arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{H\Theta}}` is equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{K\Lambda}}`.

Let us first suppose :math:`\mathrm{Z}` to be the pole of circle :math:`\mathrm{AB\Delta E}`: therefore each of the angles :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Gamma AH}`, :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Lambda E\Gamma}` is a right angle; and angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{A\Gamma E}` is equal to angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{A\Gamma H}`; and furthermore arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}` is equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma B}}`: wherefore arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma H}}` is equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma A}}`. By the like reasoning it shall be established that :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{H\Theta}} = \widehat{\mathrm{K\Lambda}}`. *Q.E.D.*

----

Proposition II.12 — Rising Times on the Oblique Horizon (pp. 78–79)
-------------------------------------------------------------------

.. admonition:: Enunciation & Conclusion of Book II (*Halley 1758, p. 79*)

   *If two great circles touch one and the same parallel circle, yet one larger than the prior parallel; nor is it necessary that its inclination be toward the same parts toward which that primary circle in which we took equal arcs inclines: then the parallel circles shall cut off unequal arcs from the great circles drawn, of which the arc nearer to the greatest of the parallels shall be less. Furthermore, those same great circles shall cut off from the greatest of the parallels unequal arcs, of which the arc nearer to the common intersection of the primary great circle and the greatest parallel shall be less than the arc more remote from the same.*

**[§2.5 · Demonstration & Converse, Halley p. 79]** Let parallel circle :math:`\mathrm{A\Delta E}` touch great circle :math:`\mathrm{AB}`, and let the greatest of the parallels be :math:`\mathrm{B\Xi X}`; on arc :math:`\mathrm{AB}` let two equal arcs :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta K}} = \widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda M}}` be taken, through whose endpoints are described parallel circles :math:`\mathrm{K\Sigma}, \mathrm{\Lambda O}, \mathrm{M\Pi}` and other great circles :math:`\mathrm{\Theta X}, \mathrm{KP}, \mathrm{\Lambda\Xi}, \mathrm{MT}`, all of which touch the same parallel circle larger than :math:`\mathrm{A\Delta E}`: I say that arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Pi O}}` is greater than arc :math:`\widehat{\Sigma\Theta}`, and arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{TX}}` is less than arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{XP}}`.

**Proof**: For in triangle :math:`\triangle \mathrm{\Theta BX}`, side :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{X\Theta}}` is greater than side :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta B}}`, but not greater than a quadrant; and on arc :math:`\mathrm{B\Theta}` two equal arcs :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta K}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda M}}` are taken, from which are drawn arcs :math:`\mathrm{KP}, \mathrm{\Lambda\Xi}, \mathrm{MT}` containing with the base angles equal to the corresponding angle at :math:`\mathrm{X}`: arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{XP}}` (*by Book II Prop. 9*) shall be greater than arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Xi T}}`; and likewise arcs :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta X}} + \widehat{\mathrm{MT}}` taken together (*by Book II Prop. 7*) shall be greater than the intermediate arcs :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{KP}} + \widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda\Xi}}`. Wherefore the two arcs :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta X}} + \widehat{\mathrm{X\Pi}}` taken together are greater than the two :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Sigma X}} + \widehat{\mathrm{XO}}` taken together: whence it is established that arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta\Sigma}} > \widehat{\mathrm{O\Pi}}`.

*And from the preceding demonstrations it is manifest how the converse of this proposition follows.*
