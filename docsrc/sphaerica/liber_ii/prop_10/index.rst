Proposition II.10 — Universal Rising-Time Theorem
-------------------------------------------------

.. admonition:: Enunciation (*Halley 1758, p. 75*)

   *If on a sphere there be a great circle touched by one of the parallel circles, and on that great circle two equal arcs be taken between the point of contact and the greatest of the parallel circles; and through the endpoints of those arcs there be described both parallel circles and great circles issuing from the pole: then those parallel circles shall cut off unequal arcs from the great circles drawn from the pole, of which the arc nearer to the greatest of the parallels shall be greater than the more remote. Moreover, that portion of the greatest of the parallels cut off by the great circles drawn from the pole which lies nearer to the common intersection of the oblique circle and the greatest parallel shall be less than the portion more remote from the same.*

**[§2.3 · Demonstration & Note, pp. 75–76]** Let :math:`\mathrm{\Theta MB}` be a great circle of the sphere, touched by one of the parallel circles :math:`\mathrm{A\Delta E}`, and let the greatest of the parallel circles be :math:`\mathrm{BT\Xi PX}`; and on :math:`\mathrm{\Theta B}` let two equal arcs :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta K}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda M}}` be taken; and through the :term:`pole <polus>` :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma}` and the points :math:`\mathrm{\Theta}, \mathrm{K}, \mathrm{\Lambda}, \mathrm{M}` let great-circle arcs :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma\Theta X}`, :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma KP}`, :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma\Lambda\Xi}`, :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma MT}` be drawn; and let parallel circles pass through points :math:`\mathrm{K}, \mathrm{\Lambda}, \mathrm{M}`, namely arcs :math:`\mathrm{K\Sigma}`, :math:`\mathrm{\Lambda O}`, :math:`\mathrm{M\Pi}`:

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0092
   :page: 76

I say that arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Pi O}}` is greater than arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Sigma\Theta}}`, and that arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{XP}}` is greater than arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Xi T}}`.

For since :math:`\triangle \mathrm{M\Gamma\Theta}` is a spherical triangle, whose leg :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{M\Gamma}}` is greater than leg :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Theta}}`, and each is less than a quadrant; and on :math:`\mathrm{\Theta M}` equal arcs :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta K}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda M}}` are taken: it follows of necessity—by what we demonstrated in **Book I Prop. 33** and **Book II Prop. 7**—that angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Theta\Gamma K}` is greater than angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Lambda\Gamma M}`, and arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{XP}}` is greater than arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Xi T}}`; and that arcs :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{M\Gamma}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Theta}}` taken together exceed :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda\Gamma}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma K}}` taken together; whence it is manifest that arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Pi O}}` is greater than arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Sigma\Theta}}`.

* **N.B.** (*p. 76*): *"Observe that this proposition comprises the 5th, 6th, and 9th of Book III of Theodosius's Sphaerica."*
