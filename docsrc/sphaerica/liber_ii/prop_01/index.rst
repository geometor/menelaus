Proposition II.1 — Problem: Oblique Spherical Angle Construction
================================================================

.. rubric:: *Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. II Prop. I (Halley 1758, p. 48)*

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0064
   :page: 48

.. admonition:: Enunciation (*Halley 1758, p. 48*)

   *From a point taken on the side of an obtuse-angled spherical triangle opposite to the obtuse angle, to draw an arc of a great circle which, with one of the arcs containing the obtuse angle, shall comprehend an angle equal to that same obtuse angle.*

**[§2.1 · Construction & Demonstration, Halley pp. 48–49]** Let :math:`\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}` be a spherical triangle such as we have described, so that :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}, \widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}` are less than a semicircle; and let point :math:`\mathrm{\Delta}` be taken on arc :math:`\mathrm{B\Gamma}`: it is required to draw from point :math:`\mathrm{\Delta}` to arc :math:`\mathrm{A\Gamma}` an arc of a great circle containing with :math:`\mathrm{A\Gamma}` itself an angle equal to the obtuse angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{BA\Gamma}`.

Let :math:`\mathrm{M}` be the :term:`pole <polus>` of arc :math:`\mathrm{A\Gamma}`, and through points :math:`\mathrm{M}, \mathrm{\Delta}` let great-circle arc :math:`\mathrm{M\Delta I}` be drawn; and produce arc :math:`\mathrm{A\Gamma}` to :math:`\mathrm{E}` so that :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{AE}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma I}}` are equal; and draw great-circle arc :math:`\mathrm{ME}`. Now since :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma B}}, \widehat{\mathrm{BA}}` together are less than a semicircle, (*by Book I Prop. 13*)

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0065
   :page: 49

angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{EAB}` will be greater than angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Gamma}`. Let angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{EAK}` (*by Book I Prop. 1*) be made equal to angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Gamma}`; and since the angles at the equal arcs of the two triangles :math:`\triangle \mathrm{\Gamma\Delta I}, \triangle \mathrm{AKE}` are respectively equal, arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{EK}}` will be equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{I\Delta}}`, and arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{EM}}` equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{MI}}`; wherefore arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{MK}}` is equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{M\Delta}}`. With pole :math:`\mathrm{M}`, therefore, and distance :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{M\Delta}}`, describe circular arc :math:`\mathrm{K\Lambda\Delta}`, meeting arc :math:`\mathrm{AB}` at point :math:`\mathrm{\Lambda}`; and draw great-circle arc :math:`\mathrm{M\Lambda Z}`. Now since angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{A}` is obtuse, :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}` will be greater than :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}`; and accordingly :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma I}}` greater than :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{AZ}}`. Place therefore on arc :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma I}` arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{I\Theta}}` equal to :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{AZ}}`, and through :math:`\mathrm{\Delta}, \mathrm{\Theta}` let great-circle arc :math:`\mathrm{\Delta\Theta}` pass: I say that angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Delta\Theta\Gamma}` is equal to angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{BA\Gamma}`.

For since arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{M\Lambda Z}}` is equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{M\Delta I}}`, and :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{M\Lambda}}` is equal to :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{M\Delta}}`, arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda Z}}` will be equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{I\Delta}}`. Moreover we made arcs :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{AZ}}, \widehat{\mathrm{I\Theta}}` equal, and angles :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Delta I\Theta}, \angle \mathrm{\Lambda ZA}` are also equal, being right: therefore angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Delta\Theta I}` (*by Book I Prop. 4*) will be equal to angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{B\Lambda Z}`: wherefore the adjacent angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Delta\Theta\Gamma}` will be equal to angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{BA\Gamma}`. *Quod erat demonstrandum.*
