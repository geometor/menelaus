Liber II: Spherical Arcs, Great Circles & Rising-Time Inequalities
==================================================================

.. rubric:: *Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Liber Secundus* — Edmond Halley (1758, pp. 48–79)

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0064
   :page: 48

Where Book I established the pure intrinsic geometry of an isolated spherical triangle, Book II (*Propositions II.1–II.12*, Halley pp. 48–79) applies those triangle inequalities to families of great circles cutting across an oblique great circle (such as the ecliptic) and systems of parallel circles (diurnal parallels to the celestial equator).

On page 75, before Proposition II.10, Menelaus delivers his famous programmatic critique of his predecessor :person:`Theodosius of Bithynia <theodosius>`:

.. epigraph::

   *"Having therefore premised these demonstrations, we have subjoined what follows in order to verify the propositions contained in the book of Theodosius On the Sphere, together with their converses—yet in a manner more universal and such as compels assent, both for establishing the first principles and for demonstrating the things written previously, some of which indeed scarcely appear sufficiently firm."*

   — :person:`Menelaus of Alexandria <menelaus>`, *Sphaerica* Book II (Halley 1758, p. 75)

Book II systematically collapses multiple tedious 3D chord cases into elegant, universal synthetic theorems derived from the intrinsic geometry of spherical triangles on :math:`S^2`.
