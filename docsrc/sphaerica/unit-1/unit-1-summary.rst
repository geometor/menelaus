Unit I — Analytical Summary: Intrinsic Spherical Foundations
============================================================

.. rubric:: *Deductive Architecture of Menelaus's Sphaerica Book I (Propositions I.1–I.38)*

1. **The Intrinsic Axiomatic Shift (Defs. I–VI)**:
   Unlike :person:`Theodosius <theodosius>`, who treated circles on a sphere solely via 3D Euclidean planes and chords, :person:`Menelaus <menelaus>` defines the :term:`spherical triangle <triangulum-sphaericum>` intrinsically on :math:`S^2` by minor great-circle arcs (:math:`< 180^\circ`) and dihedral inclinations.
2. **Neutral vs. Positively Curved Theorems (Props. I.1–I.10)**:
   Menelaus establishes spherical angle copying (I.1), isosceles base angles and converse (I.2–I.3), SAS congruence (I.4), the triangle inequality (I.5), and SSS / Hinge inequalities (I.7–I.9) without invoking parallel lines. Proposition I.10 introduces the decisive curvature threshold: the relation between an exterior angle and a remote interior angle depends on whether the two adjacent sides sum to *less than*, *equal to*, or *greater than* a semicircle.
3. **Spherical Angle-Sum Excess (Prop. I.11)**:
   By completing sides ``ΑΒ`` and ``ΑΓ`` to the antipodal vertex ``Δ`` (forming a spherical lune of two semicircles) and applying I.10, Menelaus proves directly that :math:`\angle_{\text{ext}} < \angle \mathrm{A} + \angle \mathrm{B}` and hence :math:`\angle \mathrm{A} + \angle \mathrm{B} + \angle \mathrm{\Gamma} > 2\text{ right angles}`—the historic first rigorous demonstration of positive :term:`spherical excess <excessus-sphaericus>`.
4. **AAA Spherical Congruence (Prop. I.17) & Cevian Inequalities (I.18–I.38)**:
   Without needing polar triangles (developed explicitly in the Renaissance by Viète and Snell), Menelaus constructs a vertical SAS reflection at vertex ``Β``, produces ``ΙΘ`` to ``Κ`` on ``ΑΓ``, and applies Proposition I.10 twice to equate two semicircle sums (:math:`\widehat{\mathrm{K\Theta}} + \widehat{\mathrm{K\Gamma}} = \widehat{\mathrm{IK}} + \widehat{\mathrm{KA}}`), immediately forcing :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta I}} = \widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}`! The remainder of Book I (Props. I.18–I.38) investigates medians, angle bisectors, and segments cut off on spherical arcs.
