Analytical Summary: Right-Ascension vs. Ecliptic-Longitude Ratios
=================================================================

.. rubric:: *Deductive Decomposition of Proposition III.12 (Halley 1758, pp. 99–101)*

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0115
   :page: 99

1. **Astronomical Meaning**:
   When base :math:`\mathrm{AB}` is the celestial equator, hypotenuse :math:`\mathrm{B\Gamma}` is the ecliptic (up to :math:`90^\circ`), and the perpendiculars :math:`\mathrm{\Delta E}, \mathrm{ZH}, \mathrm{\Theta K}` are declination circles meeting at the celestial pole :math:`\mathrm{\Lambda}`, Proposition III.12 compares differences in right ascension (:math:`\mathrm{AE}, \mathrm{HK}`) with differences in ecliptic longitude (:math:`\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}, \mathrm{Z\Theta}`):

   .. math::

      \frac{\widehat{\mathrm{AE}}}{\widehat{\mathrm{HK}}} \;>\; \frac{\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}}}{\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Theta}}}.

   Equal ecliptic arcs near the solstice correspond to larger right-ascension intervals than equal ecliptic arcs near the equinox.

2. **Two Proofs**:
   - **First proof** (p. 100): Direct application of Prop. III.5 (sum-and-difference sine law in right spherical triangles) together with the lost *Liber Lemmatum Cyclicorum*.
   - **Second proof** (pp. 100–101): Polar construction drawing declination circles to the pole :math:`\mathrm{\Lambda}` of the equator and applying Prop. III.3 (*Rule of Four Quantities*) repeatedly.
