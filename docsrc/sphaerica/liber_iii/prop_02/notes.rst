Scholarly & GEOMETOR Notes: Abū Naṣr Manṣūr & Halley's Corollaries
==================================================================

.. rubric:: *Historical Emergence of the Sine Rule from Menelaus III.2*

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0102
   :page: 86

1. Right-Angled Reductions (Halley p. 86, Prop. I Coroll. 2)
------------------------------------------------------------

At the top of page 86 (folio ``0102``), immediately preceding Proposition III.2, Edmond Halley appends *Coroll. 2* to the preceding transversal theorem: when :math:`\Gamma` is the pole of :math:`AB` and :math:`B` is the pole of :math:`A\Gamma`, angles :math:`A, E, \Delta` are right angles and triangles :math:`\Gamma EZ, B\Delta Z` are right-angled spherical triangles, from which *"all the canons for resolving cases of spherical trigonometry may be deduced with almost no trouble by dropping perpendiculars to reduce oblique triangles to right-angled cases."*

2. The Tenth-Century Sine Rule Revolution
-----------------------------------------

In the late 10th century CE, :person:`Abū Naṣr Manṣūr <abu-nasr-mansur>` (teacher of al-Bīrūnī), Abū al-Wafā' al-Būzjānī, and al-Khujandī extracted the **Rule of Sine** (*qānūn al-juyūb*) from Menelaus III.2, liberating spherical astronomy from constructing complete six-arc transversal figures for every calculation.
