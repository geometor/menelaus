Book III: The Transversal Theorem, Sines & Harmonic Pencils
===========================================================

.. rubric:: *Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Liber Tertius* — Edmond Halley (1758, pp. 80–112) & Claudius Ptolemy (1898, pp. 68–75)

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0096
   :page: 80

Book III is the mathematical pinnacle of the *Sphaerica* and the foundational cornerstone of spherical trigonometry in world mathematics. Where Books I and II establish the synthetic geometry of spherical triangles and rising-time inequalities, Book III develops the **exact quantitative chord/sine metric**:

1. **The Menelaus Transversal Theorem (*Theorema Menelai* / *Figura Catha*, Prop. III.1, Halley pp. 80–82)**:
   Transfers the six-segment complete quadrilateral ratio across great circles via central gnomonic projection from the sphere center to planar chord triangles.
2. **Foundations of Trigonometry (Lemmata I–II & Props. III.2–III.5)**:
   Establishes the Planar Menelaus Theorem, the Chord-Sine projection lemma, the right-spherical-triangle canon, the **Spherical Law of Sines**, and the Rule of Four Quantities and Tangents.
3. **Harmonic Pencils & Concurrencies (Props. III.6–III.10)**:
   Proves the internal and external angle-bisector theorem in sines, spherical harmonic division and 4-ray cross-ratio pencil invariance, the concurrence of angle bisectors (incenter), and the crowning three-transversal proof of the **concurrency of spherical altitudes** (orthocenter) fifteen centuries before Ceva and Poncelet.
4. **Astronomical Rising Times (Props. III.11–III.15)**:
   Concludes with exact arc-difference ratio bounds and small-circle extremum inequalities (numbered III.11–III.25 in Abū Naṣr Manṣūr's Arabic redaction) governing ancient and medieval spherical astronomy.
