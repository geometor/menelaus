Scholarly & GEOMETOR Notes: Spherical Incenter and Ceva's Concurrency
=====================================================================

.. rubric:: *From Menelaus III.9 to Ceva's Concurrency Criterion*

1. Antecedent of Ceva's Concurrency
-----------------------------------

Proposition III.9 proves that the three angle bisectors of a spherical triangle concur. Written around 100 CE, it is one of the earliest rigorous concurrence proofs for spherical cevians in the history of geometry.

Multiplying the three bisector ratios:

.. math::

   \frac{\sin\widehat{A E}}{\sin\widehat{E \Gamma}}
   \cdot
   \frac{\sin\widehat{\Gamma D}}{\sin\widehat{D B}}
   \cdot
   \frac{\sin\widehat{B F}}{\sin\widehat{F A}}
   \;=\;
   \frac{\sin c}{\sin a}
   \cdot
   \frac{\sin a}{\sin b}
   \cdot
   \frac{\sin b}{\sin c}
   \;=\; +1,

which is the exact **Spherical Ceva Condition** for internal angle bisectors!

2. Role in Proposition III.10
-----------------------------

Proposition III.9 serves as the critical intermediate step in Menelaus's proof of Proposition III.10 (concurrency of altitudes). Menelaus proves that the altitudes of triangle :math:`\triangle AB\Gamma` are the angle bisectors of its **orthic triangle** :math:`\triangle \Delta EH`, and applies Proposition III.9 to establish their concurrence!
