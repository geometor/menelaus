Lemma I — The Planar Menelaus Transversal Theorem
=================================================

.. rubric:: *Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. III Lemma I (Halley 1758, p. 83)*

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0099
   :page: 83

.. picture:: 0099.g1.svg
   :alt: Calibrated vector diagram for folio 0099.g1

.. picture:: 0099.g1.png
   :alt: Archival scan of folio 0099.g1

.. admonition:: Enunciation (*Halley 1758, p. 83*)

   *If to two straight lines* :math:`\mathrm{AB}, \mathrm{A\Gamma}` *meeting at* :math:`\mathrm{A}` *there be drawn two other straight lines* :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}, \mathrm{BE}` *intersecting one another at point* :math:`\mathrm{Z}`: *I say that straight line* :math:`\mathrm{AE}` *is to* :math:`\mathrm{E\Gamma}` *in the ratio compounded of the ratio* :math:`\mathrm{\Delta Z}` *to* :math:`\mathrm{Z\Gamma}` *and the ratio* :math:`\mathrm{BA}` *to* :math:`\mathrm{B\Delta}`.*

.. picture:: /_static/figures/menelaus-planar-parallel.svg
   :alt: Exact Planar Menelaus Theorem with auxiliary parallel CH || DF
   :caption: Exact Planar Menelaus construction (Halley Lemma I): transversal DF cuts the sides of △ABC at D (external on BC), E (on CA), and F (on AB), with auxiliary parallel CH ∥ DF.

For through :math:`\mathrm{A}` let :math:`\mathrm{AH}` be drawn parallel to :math:`\mathrm{BE}`, meeting :math:`\mathrm{\Delta\Gamma}` produced at :math:`\mathrm{H}`. Now since :math:`\mathrm{AH}` is parallel to :math:`\mathrm{EZ}`, as :math:`\mathrm{AE}` is to :math:`\mathrm{E\Gamma}`, so is :math:`\mathrm{HZ}` to :math:`\mathrm{Z\Gamma}`. And taking the straight line :math:`\mathrm{Z\Delta}` as a mean term, the ratio :math:`\mathrm{HZ}` to :math:`\mathrm{Z\Gamma}` shall be compounded of the ratio which :math:`\mathrm{HZ}` has to :math:`\mathrm{Z\Delta}` and of that which :math:`\mathrm{Z\Delta}` has to :math:`\mathrm{Z\Gamma}`. But by reason of the parallels :math:`\mathrm{AH}, \mathrm{BZ}`, as :math:`\mathrm{ZH}` is to :math:`\mathrm{Z\Delta}`, so is :math:`\mathrm{BA}` to :math:`\mathrm{B\Delta}`: therefore the ratio :math:`\mathrm{HZ}` to :math:`\mathrm{Z\Gamma}`, that is, :math:`\mathrm{AE}` to :math:`\mathrm{E\Gamma}`, is compounded of the ratio which :math:`\mathrm{BA}` has to :math:`\mathrm{B\Delta}` and of that which :math:`\mathrm{\Delta Z}` has to :math:`\mathrm{Z\Gamma}`:

.. math::

   \frac{\mathrm{AE}}{\mathrm{E\Gamma}}
   \;=\;
   \frac{\mathrm{BA}}{\mathrm{B\Delta}}
   \cdot
   \frac{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}{\mathrm{Z\Gamma}}.

With the same things posited, I say also that :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma A}` is to :math:`\mathrm{AE}` in the ratio compounded of the ratio which :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}` has to :math:`\mathrm{\Delta Z}` and of that which :math:`\mathrm{ZB}` has to :math:`\mathrm{BE}`.

Let :math:`\mathrm{E\Theta}` be drawn parallel to :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}`, and by reason of the same parallels, as :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma A}` is to :math:`\mathrm{AE}`, so is :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}` to :math:`\mathrm{E\Theta}`; let :math:`\mathrm{\Delta Z}` be taken as a mean term, and the ratio :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma A}` to :math:`\mathrm{E\Theta}` shall be compounded of that which :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}` has to :math:`\mathrm{\Delta Z}` and of that which :math:`\mathrm{\Delta Z}` has to :math:`\mathrm{E\Theta}`. But by reason of the parallels :math:`\mathrm{\Delta Z}, \mathrm{\Theta E}`, :math:`\mathrm{\Delta Z}` is to :math:`\mathrm{E\Theta}` as :math:`\mathrm{ZB}` is to :math:`\mathrm{BE}`: therefore the ratio :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma A}` to :math:`\mathrm{E\Theta}`, that is, :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma A}` to :math:`\mathrm{AE}`, is compounded of the ratio :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}` to :math:`\mathrm{\Delta Z}` and the ratio :math:`\mathrm{ZB}` to :math:`\mathrm{BE}`. *Quod erat demonstrandum.*
