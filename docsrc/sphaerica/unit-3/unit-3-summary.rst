Unit III — Analytical Summary: Gnomonic Reduction & Spherical Trigonometry
==========================================================================

.. rubric:: *Deductive Chain of Menelaus's Sphaerica Book III (Propositions III.1–III.5)*

1. **Planar Menelaus Lemma (*Lemma I*, Halley p. 83; *Almagest* I.13, Heiberg p. 69)**:
   Drawing a single auxiliary parallel (``ΑΗ ∥ ΒΕ`` or ``ΕΗ ∥ ΓΔ``) converts a complete quadrilateral (a triangle ``ΑΒΔ`` cut by a :term:`transversal <transversalis>` ``ΓΖΕ``) into two pairs of Euclid VI.4 similar triangles, compounding two simple ratios through an intermediate segment ``ΖΔ`` to give both the *katà sýnthesin* (composition) and *katà diaíresin* (division) planar product of three ratios equal to unity.
2. **Chord-Sine Projection (*Lemma II*, Halley p. 83; *Almagest* I.13, Heiberg pp. 70–72)**:
   Any radius ``ΔΒ`` from the center of a circle meets a chord ``ΑΓ`` at ``Ε`` and its arc at ``Β``, dividing the chord in the exact ratio of the perpendiculars (sines / half-chords of double arcs) dropped from ``Α`` and ``Γ`` onto diameter ``ΔΒ``: :math:`\mathrm{AE} : \mathrm{E\Gamma} = \sin \widehat{\mathrm{AB}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}} = \operatorname{crd}(2\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}) : \operatorname{crd}(2\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}})`.
3. **3D Plane-Intersection / Gnomonic Reduction (*Prop. III.1*, Halley pp. 80–82; *Almagest* I.13, Heiberg pp. 74–75)**:
   On the sphere centered at ``Β``, the plane of the transversal great circle ``ΑΘΕ`` cuts the plane of the chordal triangle ``ΝΛΜ`` along a single straight line ``ΣΔΚ`` (by Euclid *Elements* XI.3, or parallel to diameter ``ΕΒΚ`` by XI.9 in the semicircle-sum case). Applying *Lemma I* to planar triangle ``ΝΛΜ`` cut by ``ΣΔΚ`` and translating all three chordal ratios into arc-sine ratios via *Lemma II* yields **Spherical Menelaus** in one stroke!
4. **Immediate Trigonometric Consequences (*Props. III.2–III.5*)**:
   Specializing two arcs of the six-arc Menelaus configuration to quadrants gives the **right-angled spherical triangle canon** (*Prop. III.1 Coroll. 2*, p. 86), the **Spherical Law of Sines** (*Prop. III.2*, pp. 86–87—formulated intrinsically by :person:`Abū Naṣr Manṣūr <abu-nasr-mansur>` as the *Rule of Sine* freeing astronomy from full six-arc quadrilaterals), the **Rule of Four Quantities & Tangents** (*Prop. III.3*, p. 88), and sum-and-difference tangent identities (*Props. III.4–III.5*, pp. 88–90).
