Proposition I.4 — Theorem: SAS Spherical Congruence
===================================================

.. rubric:: *Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. I Prop. IV. Theor. (Halley 1758, pp. 5–6)*

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0021
   :page: 5

.. admonition:: Enunciation (*Halley 1758, p. 5*)

   *If in two spherical triangles some angle of the one be equal to an angle of the other, and the arcs containing those equal angles be equal, each to each; the two remaining arcs shall be equal. And if the two remaining arcs be equal, the angles contained by the equal arcs in either triangle shall be equal.*

Let :math:`\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}` and :math:`\triangle \mathrm{\Delta EZ}` be two spherical triangles, and let angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{B}` of triangle :math:`\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}` be equal to angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{E}` of triangle :math:`\triangle \mathrm{\Delta EZ}`, and arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}` be equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}`, and arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}` to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{EZ}}`: I say that arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}` is equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta Z}}`.

With pole :math:`\mathrm{B}` and radius :math:`\mathrm{BA}` describe arc :math:`\mathrm{AH}`; and with pole :math:`\mathrm{E}` and radius :math:`\mathrm{E\Delta}` describe arc :math:`\mathrm{\Delta\Theta}`. Because angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{B}` is equal to angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{E}`, and arc :math:`\mathrm{AH}` is described with pole :math:`\mathrm{B}` and the same radius with which arc :math:`\mathrm{\Delta\Theta}` is described with pole :math:`\mathrm{E}`, arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{AH}}` will be equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta\Theta}}` (*by Coroll. 1 of Prop. I.1*). And since arc :math:`\mathrm{B\Gamma}` passes through the pole of arc :math:`\mathrm{AH}`, it will be perpendicular to it; and in like manner arc :math:`\mathrm{EZ}` will be perpendicular to arc :math:`\mathrm{\Delta\Theta}`: and because arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{B\Gamma}}` is equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{EZ}}`, and :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{BH}}` to :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{E\Theta}}` (for each is equal to :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{AB}}`), the remaining arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{H\Gamma}}` will be equal to the remaining arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta Z}}`. Now since upon the diameters of two equal circles, of which :math:`\mathrm{AH}`, :math:`\mathrm{\Delta\Theta}` are arcs, equal segments of two equal circles stand at right angles—namely arcs :math:`\mathrm{\Theta ZE}`, :math:`\mathrm{H\Gamma B}` continued—

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0022
   :page: 6

and in both segments equal portions less than their halves are taken, namely arcs :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta Z}}`, :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma H}}`; and in equal circles equal segments :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{AH}}`, :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta\Delta}}` are held: the straight line joining points :math:`\mathrm{Z}`, :math:`\mathrm{\Delta}` will be equal (*by Theodosius II.12*) to the straight line joining points :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma}`, :math:`\mathrm{A}`; and therefore arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Delta}}` is equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma A}}`.

And if arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma A}}` be equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Delta}}`, angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{B}` will be equal to angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{E}`.

The demonstration of this is the converse of the preceding. For if arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{Z\Delta}}` be equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma A}}`, the straight line joining points :math:`\mathrm{Z}`, :math:`\mathrm{\Delta}` will be equal to the straight line joining :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma}`, :math:`\mathrm{A}`. But arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta Z}}` is equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma H}}`, each of which stands at right angles upon the diameter of the circle on which it rests; and those circles are equal: wherefore (*by Theodosius II.11*) arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta\Delta}}` is equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{AH}}`, and consequently (*by Coroll. 1 of Prop. I.1*) angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{E}` is equal to angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{B}`. *Quod erat demonstrandum.*
