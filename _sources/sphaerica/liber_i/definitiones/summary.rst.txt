Analytical Summary: The Intrinsic Axiomatic Shift
=================================================

.. rubric:: *Axiomatic Foundations of Menelaus's Sphaerica*

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0017
   :page: 1

1. **Intrinsic vs. Extrinsic Geometry**:
   Before Menelaus, Greek treatises on the sphere—notably Autolycus of Pitane (*On the Moving Sphere*) and Theodosius of Bithynia (*Sphaerica*)—treated geometric configurations on a sphere purely from an extrinsic three-dimensional viewpoint, slicing the solid sphere by Euclidean planes and examining straight chords and solid angles. Menelaus takes the revolutionary conceptual step of treating the spherical surface :math:`S^2` as a two-dimensional geometric arena endowed with its own intrinsic geodesic lines (arcs of great circles).

2. **The Minor Arc Condition (Definition II)**:
   By restricting the sides of a spherical triangle to arcs strictly less than a semicircle (:math:`< \pi`), Menelaus ensures the uniqueness of geodesic segments between vertices. If arcs equal to or greater than a semicircle were permitted, antipodal vertices would admit infinitely many joining arcs, destroying triangular uniqueness.

3. **Dihedral Angle Equivalence (Definitions III–VI)**:
   A spherical angle formed by two intersecting great-circle arcs is defined by the dihedral inclination of their containing diametral planes. Two spherical angles are equal when their planes meet with equal inclination, and a spherical angle is right when its containing planes are perpendicular.
