Preface to the Kind Reader (*Praefatio Costardi*)
=================================================

.. rubric:: *George Costard's Preface to Edmond Halley's Edition of Menelaus's Sphaerica (Oxford, 1758, pp. [i]–[viii])*

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0009
   :page: [i]

**[§1.1 · Halley 1758, pp. i–ii]** :person:`Euclid <euclid>`, who leads the column of mathematicians—edited in 1703 by the most learned David Gregory, Savilian Professor of Astronomy—was followed in 1706 by the books of Apollonius of Perga *On Cutting Off a Ratio and a Space*, translated into Latin and restored from an Arabic manuscript by a man beyond all praise, :person:`Edmond Halley <halley>`, Gregory's closest colleague; and in the very next year (1707) by the *Sphaerica* of :person:`Theodosius <theodosius>`, edited by Joseph Hunt, Master of Balliol College, followed in 1710 by Apollonius's *Conics* with the lemmas of :person:`Pappus of Alexandria <pappus>`. To the same studies and vigils of Halley the Mathematical Republic owes this our :person:`Menelaus <menelaus>`, which—though printed complete at the University Press under Halley's own care—had not yet come forth into the light.

**[§1.2 · Halley 1758, pp. ii–vi]** It is evident that our author :person:`Menelaus <menelaus>` spent his time at Rome observing the stars in the first year of the Emperor Trajan, or A.D. 97–98, whence it is clear that he preceded :person:`Ptolemy <ptolemy>`, who cites his observations (:cite:`Syntaxis VII.3 <ptolemy-almagest-vii-3>`). Since the Greek text of Menelaus has perished—or at least has not yet seen the light—two Hebrew manuscripts are preserved in the most amply furnished Bodleian Library (*Catalog. Hunting.*), bearing the title *Codex Menelai de Figuris Sphaericis ex Versione R. Isaaci Filii Honaini* (:person:`Isḥāq ibn Ḥunayn <ishaq-ibn-hunayn>`, translated into Hebrew by :person:`Jacob ben Machir <jacob-ben-machir>`), beside another codex (*Hunting. 6270. 524*) containing Theodosius, Menelaus, :person:`Thābit ibn Qurra <thabit-ibn-qurra>`, and Ibn Aflaḥ on the Sphere. That the distinguished editor :person:`Halley <halley>` also saw and made use of an Arabic version is abundantly manifest. Later, under the revival of letters, :person:`Maurolico <maurolico>`, Abbot of Messina, first brought him forth publicly speaking Latin (:cite:`1558 <maurolico-1558>`), and :person:`Mersenne <mersenne>` edited him again in his *Synopsis Mathematica* (:cite:`1644 <mersenne-1644>`), though departing farthest from what is genuine in Menelaus by unadorned propositions and added interpolations. These are the chief things which we thought it concerned you to know. Farewell, and look kindly upon our endeavors.
