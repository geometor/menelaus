Scholarly & GEOMETOR Notes: Neutral Geometry Boundaries
=======================================================

.. rubric:: *Hilbert's Non-Archimedean Axiom System & Spherical Lune Topology*

1. Overturning Neutral Geometry
-------------------------------

In David Hilbert's *Grundlagen der Geometrie* (1899), Euclid *Elements* I.16 is derived purely from Axioms of Incidence, Order, and Congruence (Axioms I–IV), without invoking Archimedes' Axiom (V.1) or the Parallel Postulate (IV).

Why does Elements I.16 fail on :math:`S^2`? Because Hilbert's Axioms of Order (Axioms II.1–II.4, Pasch's Axiom) require lines to be homeomorphic to :math:`\mathbb{R}` (infinite and open). Great circles on :math:`S^2` are homeomorphic to :math:`S^1` (closed and compact). Betweenness on :math:`S^1` is cyclic rather than linear, allowing two geodesics to cross each other twice at antipodal points :math:`\mathbf{P}` and :math:`-\mathbf{P}`.

2. Menelaus's Direct Demonstration of Elements I.25
----------------------------------------------------

Proposition I.8 establishes SSS congruence and the Hinge Theorem on the sphere. Proclus (*In Eucl.* p. 345) explicitly commends Menelaus for giving a direct constructive proof of this theorem, dispensing with Euclid's indirect *reductio ad absurdum* reduction.
