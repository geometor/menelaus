Proposition I.11 — Theorem: Spherical Angle-Sum Excess
======================================================

.. rubric:: *Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. I Prop. XI (Halley 1758, p. 11)*

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0027
   :page: 11

.. admonition:: Enunciation (*Halley 1758, p. 11*)

   *In every spherical triangle, when one side is produced, the exterior angle is less than both remote interior angles taken together; and the three interior angles of the triangle taken together are greater than two right angles.*

**[§1.4 · Demonstration]** Let :math:`\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}` be a spherical triangle: I say that the exterior angle contained by arcs :math:`\mathrm{B\Gamma}` and :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}` is less than angles :math:`\mathrm{A}` and :math:`\mathrm{B}` opposite to it; and that the three angles of the triangle :math:`\mathrm{A}, \mathrm{B}, \mathrm{\Gamma}` taken together exceed two right angles.

At point :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma}` on arc :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}` let angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Delta\Gamma E}` be made equal to angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{A}`, and produce :math:`\mathrm{AB}` to meet :math:`\mathrm{A\Gamma}` produced at point :math:`\mathrm{\Delta}`.

Now since angles :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Delta}` and :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Delta\Gamma E}` are equal, arcs :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Delta E}}` and :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{E\Gamma}}` will also be equal; and :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{BE}}` and :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{E\Gamma}}` together are equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{B\Delta}}`, and therefore are strictly **less than a semicircle** (since :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{AB}} + \widehat{\mathrm{B\Delta}}` is a semicircle, and :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{AB}} > 0`).

Wherefore (*by the preceding Proposition I.10*) the exterior angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Gamma BA}` is greater than the interior angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{B\Gamma E}`. And consequently angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{B\Gamma\Delta}` exterior to triangle :math:`\triangle \mathrm{AB\Gamma}` is less than angles :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Gamma BA}` and :math:`\angle \mathrm{BA\Gamma}` taken together.

Let the common interior angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{B\Gamma A}` be added to both sides: then the two angles :math:`\angle \mathrm{B\Gamma\Delta}` and :math:`\angle \mathrm{B\Gamma A}` are less than the three angles :math:`\angle \mathrm{A}, \angle \mathrm{B}, \angle \mathrm{\Gamma}`. But the two angles :math:`\angle \mathrm{B\Gamma\Delta}` and :math:`\angle \mathrm{B\Gamma A}` are together equal to two right angles (being adjacent on a semicircle):

Wherefore the three angles :math:`\angle \mathrm{A}, \angle \mathrm{B}, \angle \mathrm{\Gamma}` taken together strictly exceed two right angles:

.. math::

   A + B + C \;>\; \pi.

*Quod erat demonstrandum.*
