Book I: Intrinsic Spherical Triangles, Congruence & Excess
==========================================================

.. rubric:: *Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Liber Primus* — Edmond Halley (1758, pp. [i]–[viii], 1–47)

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0017
   :page: 1

In Book I, :person:`Menelaus of Alexandria <menelaus>` creates the intrinsic axiomatic science of spherical trigonometry on :math:`S^2`. Moving decisively beyond the three-dimensional solid-geometry and plane-slicing methods of :person:`Theodosius of Bithynia <theodosius>`, Menelaus defines a **spherical triangle** (:term:`triangulum sphaericum <triangulum-sphaericum>`) directly upon the curved surface of the sphere as a space enclosed by three arcs of great circles, each strictly less than a semicircle (:term:`circulus magnus <circulus-magnus>`).

Through an elegant sequence of direct constructions and demonstrations—avoiding *reductio ad absurdum* and planar superposition fallacies—Menelaus proves:

* **Copying Spherical Angles** (:ref:`Proposition I.1 <liber-i-prop-1>`): polar circle construction without straightedge superposition.
* **Congruence Theorems** (:ref:`Propositions I.4, I.8, I.14–16, I.17 <liber-i-prop-4-5>`): SAS, SSS, ASA, and the celebrated **AAA Spherical Congruence Theorem** (:ref:`Proposition I.17 <liber-i-prop-17>`), establishing that on a positively curved surface, equiangular triangles are strictly congruent with zero non-congruent scale dilation.
* **Spherical Triangle Inequality** (:ref:`Proposition I.5 <liber-i-prop-4-5>`): any two great-circle arcs together exceed the third.
* **Spherical Angle-Sum Excess** (:ref:`Proposition I.11 <liber-i-prop-11>`): the historic first rigorous demonstration that the angle sum of an intrinsic spherical triangle strictly exceeds two right angles (:math:`A + B + C > \pi`).
