Propositions III.11–III.25 — Astronomical Rising Times & Closing Horizon
========================================================================

.. rubric:: *Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. III Props. XI–XXV (Halley 1758, pp. 97–112)*

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0113
   :page: 97

The Final Division of Book III (pp. 97–112)
-------------------------------------------

Following the triumph of Proposition III.10 (concurrency of altitudes), the remainder of Book III (*Propositions III.11–III.25*, Halley pp. 97–112) shifts from triangle center configurations to quantitative spherical difference-ratio bounds and rising-time proportion laws:

Proposition III.11 — Base Arc Differences & Ratio Bounds (p. 97)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

.. admonition:: Enunciation (*Halley 1758, p. 97*)

   *If the greater leg of a spherical triangle does not exceed a quadrant of a circle, and from that greater leg two arcs be taken, from whose endpoints arcs be drawn to the base containing with it angles equal to the angle contained by the base and the remaining leg; and if the arcs taken be equal: the differences between those drawn arcs shall be unequal, and the less of the differences shall be that between the arcs adjacent to the less leg... And universally the ratio of that arc taken nearer to the vertex of the triangle to the remaining more remote arc shall be greater than the ratio which the difference between the arcs drawn through the endpoints of the prior has to the difference which is between those drawn through the endpoints of the latter.*

----

Propositions III.12–III.25 — Astronomical Application Horizon (pp. 98–112)
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

The final fourteen propositions systematically translate the quantitative metric of Proposition III.1 into exact astronomical inequalities:

* **Rising Times on the Oblique Sphere (ἀναφοραί)**:
  Calculating the duration required for successive signs and degrees of the ecliptic to cross the eastern horizon at a given terrestrial latitude.
* **Meridian Altitudes & Solstitial Declinations**:
  Bounding the rates of change of solar and stellar declination relative to right ascension.
* **Universal Parameter Tables**:
  Providing the synthetic geometric framework that underpinned Claudius Ptolemy's *Almagest* tables, al-Battānī's *Zīj*, Thābit ibn Qurra's treatises on the sector figure, and Edmond Halley's Oxford edition.
