Scholarly & GEOMETOR Notes: Abū Naṣr Manṣūr and the Discovery of the Sine Rule
==============================================================================

.. rubric:: *Historical Emergence of the Sine Rule from Menelaus III.2*

1. The Tenth-Century Transformation
-----------------------------------

In the late 10th century CE (c. 1000 CE), Persian and Central Asian mathematicians realized that Proposition III.2 of Menelaus's *Sphaerica* contained an autonomous trigonometric principle:

* :person:`Abū Naṣr Manṣūr <abu-nasr-mansur>` (teacher of al-Bīrūnī), **Abū al-Wafā' al-Būzjānī**, and **al-Khujandī** independently claimed discovery of the **Rule of Four Quantities** (*qānūn al-arbaʿa al-mutanāsiba*) and the **Rule of Sine** (*qānūn al-juyūb*).
* By isolating :math:`\frac{\sin a}{\sin A} = \frac{\sin b}{\sin B}`, Islamic astronomers liberated practical spherical astronomy from having to construct complete six-arc Menelaus quadrilaterals (*al-shakl al-qaṭṭāʿ*) for every calculation.

2. Halley's Evaluation
----------------------

On page 86, Edmond Halley underscores that all the canons for solving oblique and right spherical triangles flow directly from Corollary 2 and Proposition III.2, providing the foundation for Regiomontanus's *De Triangulis Omnimodis* (1464) and modern spherical trigonometry.
