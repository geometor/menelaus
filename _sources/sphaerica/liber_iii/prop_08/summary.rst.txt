Analytical Summary: Spherical Harmonic Division & Projective Invariance
========================================================================

.. rubric:: *Modern Elements-2.0 Decomposition of Proposition III.8*

1. Geometric Architecture of Harmonic Division on S²
-----------------------------------------------------

* **Direct Theorem**:
  - Right-angled spherical triangle :math:`\triangle AB\Gamma` (:math:`\angle B = 90^\circ`).
  - Two rays :math:`B\Delta, BE` making equal angles with leg :math:`BA` (:math:`\angle AB\Delta = \angle ABE`).
  - By Prop. III.6 applied to internal and external bisectors, the base points :math:`\Gamma, E, \Delta, A` satisfy:

    .. math::

       \frac{\sin\widehat{\Gamma E}}{\sin\widehat{EA}}
       \;=\;
       \frac{\sin\widehat{\Gamma\Delta}}{\sin\widehat{\Delta A}}.

* **First Converse (Characterization of Right Angles)**:
  - If the sine cross-ratio is harmonic and :math:`\angle EBA = \angle AB\Delta`, then :math:`\angle AB\Gamma = 90^\circ`.
* **Second Converse (Characterization of Bisectors)**:
  - If the sine cross-ratio is harmonic and :math:`\angle AB\Gamma = 90^\circ`, then :math:`\angle EBA = \angle AB\Delta`.

2. The Bridge to Projective Geometry
------------------------------------

Proposition III.8 is the exact spherical predecessor of harmonic bundles and the cross-ratio invariance of 4-ray pencils. Menelaus establishes that harmonic division on the sphere is purely angular: whether measured along the base arc :math:`A\Gamma` or projected via great circles, the cross-ratio in sines remains invariant.
