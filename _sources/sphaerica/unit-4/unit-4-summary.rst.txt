Unit IV — Analytical Summary: Harmonic Pencils, Concurrencies & Polarity
========================================================================

.. rubric:: *Deductive Architecture of Menelaus's Sphaerica Book III (Propositions III.6–III.25)*

1. **Spherical Bisectors & Harmonic Division (*Props. III.6–III.8*, Halley pp. 91–95)**:
   From the Spherical Law of Sines (Prop. III.2), Menelaus derives both the internal and external spherical angle-bisector theorems (Prop. III.6). Combining orthogonal internal and external bisectors at the right angle of a right spherical triangle yields **Proposition III.8**: the exact spherical :term:`harmonic conjugate <sectio-harmonica>` proportion :math:`\sin \widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma E}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{EA}} = \sin \widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Delta}} : \sin \widehat{\mathrm{\Delta A}}` together with two sharp converses characterizing right angles and bisectors via harmonic cross-ratios—generalized in :person:`Pappus <pappus>`'s *Synagoge* VI/VII (:cite:`ed. Hultsch, 1877 <pappus-hultsch-1877>`) to full 4-ray cross-ratio pencil invariance.
2. **Spherical Incenter & Orthocenter Concurrencies (*Props. III.9–III.10*, Halley pp. 95–97)**:
   - **Prop. III.9 (Incenter)**: Equating two bisector sine ratios across a common cevian ``ΒΔΕ`` proves that the three spherical angle bisectors concur at the center ``Δ`` of the inscribed small circle.
   - **Prop. III.10 (Orthocenter)**: Fifteen centuries before :person:`Giovanni Ceva <ceva>` (:cite:`1678 <ceva-1678>`), :person:`Lazare Carnot <carnot>` (:cite:`1806 <carnot-1806>`), and :person:`Jean-Victor Poncelet <poncelet>` (:cite:`1822 <poncelet-1822>`), Menelaus constructs the **orthic axis** (:term:`trilinear polar <polaris-trilinearis>`) ``ΕΔΘ`` of the altitude intersection ``Ζ``, applies **Spherical Menelaus (Prop. III.1) three times** to prove harmonic division ``(Α, Γ; Η, Θ)``, identifies ``Ζ`` as the **incenter of the orthic spherical triangle** ``ΔΕΗ`` via Prop. III.8 and Prop. III.9, and deduces ``ΒΗ ⊥ ΑΓ`` by the converse of Prop. III.8!
3. **Astronomical Difference Proportions (*Props. III.11–III.25*, Halley pp. 97–112)**:
   Book III concludes with fine-grained arc-difference ratios along meridian and horizon circles, completing the mathematical engine of ancient and medieval spherical astronomy.
