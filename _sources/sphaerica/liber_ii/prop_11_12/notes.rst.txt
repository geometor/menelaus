Scholarly & GEOMETOR Notes: The Architecture of Book II & Transition to Book III
=================================================================================

.. rubric:: *Philological Notes on Propositions II.11–12 and Book II Architecture*

1. Culmination of the Synthetic Horizon Theory
----------------------------------------------

Propositions II.11 and II.12 form the deductive summit of Book II:

* In **Halley's Latin text** (Oxford, 1758, pp. 76–79), Proposition XI provides the foundational equinoctial symmetry, while Proposition XII generalizes the rising-time theorem of Proposition X to arbitrary oblique horizons.
* In **Ptolemy's Almagest** Book II (*Syntaxis Mathematica*, ed. Heiberg I, pp. 91–105), Ptolemy addresses the very same rising-time problems (*ascensiones obliquae*), but relies on numerical chord calculations using the quantitative transversal theorem.
* Menelaus's unique achievement in Book II is providing a purely geometric, synthetic proof of these rising-time inequalities *prior to and independent of* any numerical table of chords.

2. Manuscript Witnesses and Proposition 12 Incomplete Lemmas
------------------------------------------------------------

As noted by :person:`Axel Anthon Björnbo <bjornbo>` (:cite:`1902, §8 <bjornbo-1902>`):

* In Gerard of Cremona's medieval Latin text from the Arabic, Proposition 12 is followed by lemmas clarifying the oblique horizon contacts.
* Halley's text notes at the end of page 79: *"Et ex praecedentibus manifestum est quomodo consequatur conversa hujus. MENE[LAI]..."*, immediately concluding Book II.
* The bold capital heading *"MENELAI ALEXANDRINI SPHAERICORUM Lib. III"* opens on page 80 (folio 0096).
