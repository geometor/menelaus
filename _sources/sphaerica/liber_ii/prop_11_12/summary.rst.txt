Analytical Summary: Polar Symmetries & Oblique Rising-Time Bounds
=================================================================

.. rubric:: *Modern Elements-2.0 Decomposition of Propositions II.11 & II.12*

1. Proposition II.11 — Symmetry of Great-Circle Projections
------------------------------------------------------------

* **Core Principle**: Great circles passing through the pole of an initial great circle project congruent intervals between equal arcs symmetrically placed about their node:
  - If :math:`\widehat{AB} = \widehat{\Delta E}` are symmetrically situated relative to node :math:`\Gamma`, then their projected arcs on the second circle :math:`\widehat{H\Theta} = \widehat{K\Lambda}`.
* **Astronomical Implication**: Symmetrical solar longitude arcs about the equinox project equal right-ascension increments on the celestial equator.

2. Proposition II.12 — Oblique Horizon Bounds & Conclusion of Book II
---------------------------------------------------------------------

* **Geometric Scope**: Generalizes Proposition II.10 from right ascensions (orthogonal projections relative to the celestial equator) to **oblique ascensions** (ἀναφοραί) on an arbitrary horizon touching a parallel circle of higher latitude.
* **Inequality Properties**:
  - The diurnal parallel separation nearer to the equator is smaller than the separation nearer to the solstitial limit: :math:`\widehat{\Theta\Sigma} > \widehat{O\Pi}`.
  - The intercepted horizon arc nearer to the node is smaller than the arc more remote: :math:`\widehat{\Xi T} < \widehat{XP}`.
* **Transition to Book III**:
  With Proposition II.12 and its converse established, Menelaus completes his synthetic, qualitative theory of spherical triangles and rising-time inequalities. Book III immediately follows, shifting from qualitative arc inequalities to the **exact quantitative chord/sine metric** of the *Theorema Menelai*.
