Scholarly & GEOMETOR Notes: Theodosius III.5, 6, 9 & the Arabic Transmission
=============================================================================

.. rubric:: *Philological & Historical Context of Menelaus II.10*

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0091
   :page: 75

1. Halley's Editorial Annotation
--------------------------------

On page 76, Edmond Halley appends his concise note:

.. epigraph::

   *"NB. Hanc propositionem Vtam & VIam & IXam Lib. III. Sphaericorum Theodosii complecti."*

This single proposition supersedes:

* **Theodosius III.5**: Differences in right ascension for equal ecliptic arcs.
* **Theodosius III.6**: Rising-time bounds near the equinox versus near the solstice.
* **Theodosius III.9**: Declination inequalities for equal intervals of longitude.

Where Theodosius of Bithynia (*Sphaerica*, c. 100 BCE; :cite:`ed. Hunt, 1707 <theodosius-hunt-1707>`) required elaborate spatial chord projections from the sphere center to equatorial and solstitial planes, Menelaus establishes the entire theory through spherical triangle inequalities on :math:`S^2`.

2. The Lost Greek Text and the Arabic/Hebrew Witnesses
------------------------------------------------------

As analyzed by :person:`Axel Anthon Björnbo <bjornbo>` (:cite:`1902, §§7–9 <bjornbo-1902>`), the methodological pause on page 75 is preserved across both major translation lines:

* **Ishāq ibn Hunayn** (revised by Thābit ibn Qurra) in the Arabic tradition.
* **Jacob ben Machir Ibn Tibbon** in the 1273 Hebrew version.

Both traditions record Menelaus's critique of Theodosius in virtually identical wording, confirming that this programmatic declaration originated with Menelaus in 1st-century CE Alexandria.
