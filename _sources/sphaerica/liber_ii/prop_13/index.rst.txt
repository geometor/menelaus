Proposition II.13 — Theorem: Oblique Arcs Touching a Larger Parallel
====================================================================

.. rubric:: *Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. II Prop. XIII (Halley 1758, pp. 78–79)*

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0094
   :page: 78

.. admonition:: Enunciation (*Halley 1758, pp. 78–79*)

   *If on a sphere there be a great circle touched by one of the parallel circles, and between the point of contact and the greatest of the parallels two equal arcs be taken upon it, through whose endpoints circles be drawn, of which some are parallels and others are great circles which*

   .. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0095
      :page: 79

   *touch one and the same parallel circle, yet larger than the prior parallel; nor is it necessary that its inclination be toward the same parts toward which that primary circle in which we took equal arcs inclines: then the parallel circles shall cut off from the great circles drawn unequal arcs, of which that nearer to the greatest of the parallels shall be less than the more remote. The same great circles shall also cut off from the greatest of the parallels unequal arcs, of which that nearer to the common intersection of the primary great circle and the greatest of the parallels shall be less than the more remote from the same.*

Let one of the parallel circles, namely :math:`\mathrm{A\Delta E}`, touch great circle :math:`\mathrm{AB}`; and let the greatest of the parallels be :math:`\mathrm{B\Xi X}`; and on arc :math:`\mathrm{AB}` let two equal arcs :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta K}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda M}}` be taken; through whose endpoints let parallel circles :math:`\mathrm{K\Sigma}, \mathrm{\Lambda O}, \mathrm{M\Pi}`, and other great circles :math:`\mathrm{\Theta X}, \mathrm{KP}, \mathrm{\Lambda\Xi}, \mathrm{MT}`, be described, all of which touch one and the same parallel circle larger than circle :math:`\mathrm{A\Delta E}`: I say that arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Pi O}}` is less than arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Sigma\Theta}}`, and that arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{T\Xi}}` is less than :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{XP}}`.

For since in triangle :math:`\triangle \mathrm{\Theta BX}` side :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{X\Theta}}` is greater than side :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta B}}`, yet not greater than a quadrant of a circle, and on arc :math:`\mathrm{B\Theta}` two equal arcs :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta K}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda M}}` are taken, from which arcs :math:`\mathrm{KP}, \mathrm{\Lambda\Xi}, \mathrm{MT}` are drawn containing with the base angles equal to the angle at :math:`\mathrm{X}` relative to them: arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{XP}}` (*by Book II Prop. 9*) will be greater than arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Xi T}}`; and likewise arcs :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta X}}, \widehat{\mathrm{MT}}` taken together (*by what was demonstrated in Book II Prop. 7*) will be greater than the intermediate arcs :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{KP}}, \widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda\Xi}}`: wherefore the two arcs :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta X}}, \widehat{\mathrm{X\Pi}}` taken together are greater than the two :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Sigma X}}, \widehat{\mathrm{XO}}` taken together: whence it will be established that arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta\Sigma}}` is greater than arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{O\Pi}}`. And from the preceding it is manifest how the converse of this follows.
