Proposition II.11 — Theorem: Symmetry of Polar Interceptions
============================================================

.. rubric:: *Menelai Alexandrini Sphaericorum Lib. II Prop. XI (Halley 1758, pp. 76–77)*

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0092
   :page: 76

.. picture:: 0092.g1.svg
   :alt: Calibrated vector diagram for folio 0092.g1

.. picture:: 0092.g1.png
   :alt: Archival scan of folio 0092.g1

.. admonition:: Enunciation (*Halley 1758, p. 76*)

   *Let two great circles on a sphere intersect one another, and on one of them let two arcs be taken equidistant from the point of intersection of those two circles; and through the endpoints of these arcs, and through the poles of either of the circles, let great circles pass: these circles shall cut off equal arcs from the other of the first circles.*

Let :math:`\mathrm{ABE}`, :math:`\mathrm{H\Theta\Lambda}` be two great circles of the sphere intersecting one another at point :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma}`, and on one of them let arcs :math:`\mathrm{AB}`, :math:`\mathrm{\Delta E}` be taken equidistant from point :math:`\mathrm{\Gamma}`; and let great-circle arcs be drawn passing through points :math:`\mathrm{A}, \mathrm{B}, \mathrm{\Delta}, \mathrm{E}` and through :math:`\mathrm{Z}`, the :term:`pole <polus>` of either of the circles :math:`\mathrm{ABE}`, :math:`\mathrm{H\Gamma\Lambda}`; let these arcs be :math:`\mathrm{ZAH}`, :math:`\mathrm{ZB\Theta}`, :math:`\mathrm{ZK\Delta}`, :math:`\mathrm{Z\Lambda E}`: I say that arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{H\Theta}}` is equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{K\Lambda}}`.

Let us first suppose :math:`\mathrm{Z}` to be the pole of circle :math:`\mathrm{AB\Delta E}`; wherefore each of the angles :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Gamma AH}`, :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Lambda E\Gamma}` will be a right angle. And angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{A\Gamma E}` is equal to angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{A\Gamma H}`; and also arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}` is equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma E}}`: wherefore arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma H}}` is equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Lambda}}`. By the like argument

.. folio:: halley_1758_menelai_sphaericorum:0093
   :page: 77

it shall be established that arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta\Gamma}}` is equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{K\Gamma}}`; the remaining arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta H}}` is therefore equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{K\Lambda}}`.

Now let us suppose point :math:`\mathrm{Z}` to be the pole of circle :math:`\mathrm{\Lambda\Gamma H}`; and the two angles :math:`\angle \mathrm{AH\Gamma}`, :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Gamma\Lambda E}` will be right angles. Moreover angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{A\Gamma H}` is equal to angle :math:`\angle \mathrm{\Lambda\Gamma E}`, as arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{A\Gamma}}` is equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma E}}`; and the two arcs :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{AH}}`, :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Lambda E}}` taken together are not equal to a semicircle, since each is less than a quadrant: therefore arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{H\Gamma}}` (*by Book I Prop. 16*) is equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma\Lambda}}`. And in the like manner it shall be demonstrated that arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta\Gamma}}` is equal to arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Gamma K}}`: therefore the remaining arc :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{\Theta H}}` is equal to :math:`\widehat{\mathrm{K\Lambda}}`. *Quod erat demonstrandum.*
