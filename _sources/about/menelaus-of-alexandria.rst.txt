Menelaus of Alexandria (*c.* 70–140 CE): Life, Works & Roman Observations
=========================================================================

Biographical & Astronomical Anchor (98 CE)
------------------------------------------

:person:`Menelaus of Alexandria <menelaus>` (*Μενέλαος ὁ Ἀλεξανδρεύς*, as :person:`Pappus of Alexandria <pappus>` styles him in :cite:`Synagoge VI <pappus-hultsch-1877>`) stands at the watershed between Hellenistic pure geometry and Imperial mathematical astronomy. Our firmest chronological anchor comes directly from :person:`Claudius Ptolemy <ptolemy>`'s *Syntaxis Mathematica* (:cite:`Almagest VII.3 <ptolemy-almagest-vii-3>`), which records two precision stellar occultation observations made by Menelaus at **Rome** in the first year of Emperor Trajan (*98 CE*):

1. On **15–16 Tybi** (January 98 CE), Menelaus observed the star **Spica** (*α Virginis*) occulted by the Moon and emerging along the Moon's western limb.
2. A few nights later, on **18–19 Tybi**, Menelaus measured the alignment of the northernmost star in the brow of **Scorpius** (*β Scorpii*) with the Moon's southern horn.

Plutarch (*De facie in orbe lunae*, 930A) likewise introduces the mathematician "*Menelaus*" as an interlocutor in a philosophical-optical dialogue set at Rome, apologizing playfully to him before questioning the equality of angles of incidence and reflection.

Corpus of Mathematical Writings
-------------------------------

Ancient Greek commentators and medieval Arabic bibliographers attest a wide mathematical output by Menelaus, of which only the *Sphaerica* survives intact:

- ***Sphaerica* (*Σφαιρικά* / *Sphaericorum Libri III*)**: Lost in its continuous Greek original save for fragments in :person:`Pappus <pappus>` and :person:`Theon of Alexandria <theon>`, yet preserved completely in medieval Arabic, Hebrew, and Latin translations culminating in :person:`Edmond Halley <halley>`'s critical Oxford edition (:cite:`1758 <halley-1758>`).
- ***On Chords in a Circle* (6 books)**: Attested by :person:`Theon of Alexandria <theon>` (*Commentary on Almagest I.10*), who reports that :person:`Hipparchus <hipparchus>` wrote twelve books on chords in a circle and Menelaus six.
- ***Elements of Geometry* (3 books)** & **Direct Proof of Euclid I.25**: :person:`Proclus <proclus>` (:cite:`In Euclidem p. 345 <proclus-friedlein-1873>`) preserves word-for-word Menelaus's elegant direct proof of Euclid *Elements* I.25 (the converse Hinge Theorem), deliberately eliminating Euclid's indirect *reductio ad absurdum*—the exact methodological signature visible across all three books of the *Sphaerica*.
- **Hydrostatics & Higher Curves**: Arabic treatises preserve investigations by Menelaus into specific gravities of alloyed bodies and into paradoxical curves (including Viviani-type spherical intersections).
